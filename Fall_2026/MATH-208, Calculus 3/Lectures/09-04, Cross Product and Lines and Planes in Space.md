Remember:
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Dot product geometric definition</mark> is $v\cdot w=||v||||w||\cos \theta$
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Cross product magnitude definition</mark> $||v\times w||=||v||||w||\sin \theta$

If they ask you to find the tangent of something, remember tan is sin/cos so the cross product magnitude / dot product. Theta is just the arctan of that fraction

For the <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">wrench problems</mark>, you need a position vector (r) and a force vector (F)
- If it gives you the length of the wrench, write it out as a vector. So if it's 0.5 meters going in the positive x direction, it's <0.5, 0, 0>.
- If you are given a direction vector, you can turn that into the force vector by normalizing it (find it's magnitude and divide it by that). Note that it will have an unknown scalar (the force needed to achieve a certain amount of torque) associated with it since the actual force vector does not have a magnitude of 1 like unit vectors do
	- If you are given torque and told to solve for force, you can put a temp scalar on this vector, do the cross product step, and then solve for this scalar. Remember to pull the scalar OUT when doing the cross product, it won't matter because of the geometric properties defined above
- Calculate the cross product of the direction vector and force vector and this is the torque
