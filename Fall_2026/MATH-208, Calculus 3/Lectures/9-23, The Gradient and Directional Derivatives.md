$$
gradf=\left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial p},\frac{\partial f}{\partial q},\frac{\partial f}{\partial r} \right\rangle 
$$
so on and so forth for all the variables

The magnitude of $\nabla f$ is the measure of the rate of change in the direction of $\nabla f$ which is the rate of greatest increase. $f(x_{0},y_{0})*u=|\nabla f(x_{0},y_{0})|$ so the gradient function times the unit vector in the direction of it is equal to the magnitude of the gradient function. **This gives you the rate of change per unit of movement**. If you have more units of movement, just multiply

The gradient points in the direction of steepest ascent. The gradient is orthogonal to the level curve.

The quantity is maximized when $u$ and $\nabla f$ are the same. The quantity is minimized when they are in the opposite direction presumably
