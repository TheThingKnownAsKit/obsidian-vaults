Four types of systems: information, embedded, software-intensive, and adaptive
- Info is like business grade
- Embedded is what it is
- Software-intensive is a combination of embedded and info with complex functioning and control loops
- Adaptive adds additional adaptation logic (MAPE-K) to monitor the context. Basically real time software-intensive. Takes in current environment context to modify behavior

< Mid 90s, separation between embedded and info. Mid 90s integration. > 2010 self adaptive systems. More complicated demand

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Poor requirements engineering is credited with 48.1% of challenged projects overstepping on resources</mark>

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">requirement</mark> is either
1. A need or desire perceived by a stakeholder to solve a problem or achieve an objective
2. A capability or property that a system shall offer
3. A condition the system or its development shall satisfy

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Requirements engineering is a set of activities</mark> concerned with discovering, clarifying, analyzing, communicating, and managing the evolution of stakeholders' needs, use context, and externally visible behaviors and qualities.
1. Project initiation - define scope and goals
2. Elicitation - discover stakeholders' real needs
3. Decisions
4. Documenting Artefacts
5. Validation and verification
6. Management

There are a lot of ways to model requirements
- Goal model
- Conceptual model
- Scenarios / Journey maps
- Context diagram
