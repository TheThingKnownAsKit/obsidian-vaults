## Accounting Rules
**Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)** is the general guidelines for accounting. It ensures a company's financial statements clearly represent its economic condition and performance
Reference chapter 1-4 for rules picture

Who makes rules?
**Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB)** is the rule setter 
**Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** is the enforcer of public companies
**Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB)** set the audit standards and oversee auditors of public companies

## Financial Statements
Balance sheets are very surface level. Just a snapshot

More in depth statements:
* Income statement
* Stockholder's Equity Statement
* Statement of Cash Flows
### Balance Sheet
**Assets** are economic resources owned by a company that has probable future economic benefit
* Classification: current vs long-term (1 year)
* Measurement: Historical cost (mostly historical, what you paid for it) or market value (usually just investments get updated at market value)
* Presentation: List in order of liquidity
**Liability** is owing money or goods or services at some future time for past transaction/event
* Classification: current vs non-current (1 year)
* Presentation: List in order of what needs to be paid first
**Stockholders' equity** ownership claims on assets of the business
* Benefit of being a creditor?
	* Interest payments - fixed amount
	* Paid first if bankruptcy
* Benefit of being a stockholder?
	* Capital gains (stock price increase) + dividends / fluctuates 


### Income Statement
An **income statement** is what you've earned, not necessarily what you currently have, minus what you've used/expenses
If you perform a service, and you're waiting for the bank to process the credit card payment, you still earned that money even if you don't have it right now

### Stockholders' Equity / Statement of Stockholders' Equity
Retained earnings = net income - dividends
**Retained earnings** is the profit kept in the business. Every year it goes up from the new profit made. Total profit over all time

* Contributed Capital:
	* Common Stock
	* Additional Paid in Capital
* Earned Capital
	* Retained Earnings

### Cash Flow Statement
Three categories:
* Investing Activities
	* (Property Plant Equipment) PPE buy or sold
	* Business acquisition, property, investments in other companies
* Financing Activities
	* Debt
	* Stock
		* Issue stock
		* Pay Dividends
* Operating Activities
	* Day-to-day operations
	* Customers, suppliers, etc
These all deal with CASH TRANSACTIONS. It is the flow of money in and out of the business
