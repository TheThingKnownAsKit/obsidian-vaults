**The purpose of accrual accounting is to match revenue and expenses in the same accounting period**

**Revenue recognition principle** is recognizing revenue when contractual obligation is satisfied by providing goods or services to a customer in the amount expected to be received
Revenue related accounts:
* Accounts Receivable (asset)
* Deferred or Unearned Revenue (Liability)
* Cash (Asset)

**Expense Recognition**: Recognize expense when related revenue is earned
immediate benefit vs future benefit can change how something is recorded
For example:
* Renting space is an expense but buying a building is an asset and it's expense is recorded as depreciation over the life of the building
Expense related accounts:
* Prepaid expense (asset) (cash down/expense up, prepaid up)
* Supplies/Inventory (asset) (cash down, supplies up)
* Property, plant and equipment (cash down, PPE up -> depr exp up, PPE down)
* Accounts payable for any type of interest payable (expense up, AP up)
* Cash

# T-ACCOUNT & JOURNAL ENTRY TIPS
* Normal balance is the side on which increases are recorded
* *Unlike* the financial statement transaction template,
	* You don't have T-Accounts for Net income
	* You don't record transactions during year to retained earnings in addition to revenue or expense
	* You only record to retained earnings when you close accounts at end of year
	* You don't keep track of cash flow activity
