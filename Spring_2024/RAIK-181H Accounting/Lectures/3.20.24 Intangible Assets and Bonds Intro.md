# BONDS
Basically, you get regular interest payments until the due date of the bond and then you get the original principal amount back

Bonds are similar to stock except they're classified as debt instead of equity

ADVANTAGES
* No dilution of ownership interest
* Interest expense is tax deductible. Dividends are not
* Income to common shareholders can increase
DISADVANTAGES
* Interest payments are required
	* (dividends are optional for stock, it is NOT optional on bonds)
* Funds borrowed have specific repayment dates
* Loan agreements can restrict certain activities (loan covenants)

TYPES OF BONDS
* Secured bonds
* Serial bonds
* Debenture bonds
* Convertible bonds
	* Can switch from being a debt holder to a stock holder
* Zero coupon bonds
	* No regular interest payments, you pay it all at once at the end including interest

BOND FEATURES
* Bond ratings
	* AA, A, B, C, D. How risky is the bond? Lower ratings are higher risk with higher interest rates
* Call provision
* Sinking fund

PRINCIPAL
* Face amount on sheet
* Amount that is repaid at the end of the term
* Stated on the bond

BOND PRICE
* Present value of the interest payments and the present value of the lump sum payment at the end of the term based on the market rate

COUPON RATE
* Stated rate
* Stated on the bond
* Determines interest payment
* Doesn't change

MARKET RATE
* Yield rate
* Effective rate
* Constantly changing based on the market
* Used to calculate present value

ANNUITY
* Interest payments
* Typically semi-annual

Because the coupon rate goes down, these all end up being roughly the same in the end because they will get less/more interest. The difference is the amount of cash you get upfront
