# Business Tax Structures
**Pass Through Entities** are the business structures where profits flow through to the owners. The owners pay taxes.
Pass Through Entities:
1. **Sole proprietorship**
	1. Unincorporated owned by individual
	2. Everything in owner's name
	3. Owner has all liability
	4. Advantages:
		1. Easy to start
		2. Single taxation
		3. Limited government oversight
	5. Disadvantages:
		1. Unlimited liability
		2. Difficult to transfer ownership
		3. Lack of ability to raise funds (banks don't like, etc)
2. **Partnerships**
	1. 2 or more individual owners
		1. Get a partner agreement (responsibilities, income/loss divided)
	2. Advantages:
		1. Single taxation
		2. Easy to start
		3. Skills of multiple people
	3. Disadvantages:
		1. Unlimited liability
		2. Difficult to transfer ownership
		3. Lack of ability to raise funds
3. **S Corp OR Limited Liability corp (LLC)**
	1. Advantages:
		1. Limited liability
		2. Single taxation
		3. Easy to create
	2. Disadvantages:
		1. Limited number of stockholders
		2. Specific tax rules for these

**Corporate** business structures are ones in which the business pays taxes.
The Corporate Taxpayer
1. C Corp
	1. Two types, private and publicly traded (is it on the stock market / can you see their financial statements)
	2. Advantage:
		1. Ability to raise funds (stock)
		2. Limited liability
		3. Easy transferability
	3. Disadvantages:
		1. Heavy regulation / government oversight
		2. Double taxation
			1. The business and owners (including stockholder) pay taxes on profit
		3. 3 tiers of management
			1. Owner(s)
			2. Board of directors (BOD)
			3. Management

# Financial Statements
## Balance Sheet
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets can be current or long-term.
Current is anything that is either already cash or should be converted into cash within one year.
Assets are also anything that provides future benefit to generate more earnings (cars)

Liabilities can be current or long-term.
Current is anything they owe within the year. These are called creditors.

**Working capital** is how much money you have to work with this year.
$Current Asset - Current Liability = Working Capital$

Equity is things such as common stock, additional paid in capital, and retained earnings. Retained earnings are funds invested by stockholders or accumulated in earnings in

Equity and liabilities are the funding sources.

Always check the rounding number (millions vs thousands) and when the end of the year is for the company.

A lot of assets use **historical cost**. This is how much you paid for it, not how much it is currently worth. Exceptions are things like investment accounts, you would record how much is in the account.

### WEIRD TERMS
Receivables / account receivables are people who owe the company money. Wording like "less allowances of $800 and $692" just means they never expect those people to pay them since bankruptcy

Goodwill means the company bought another company and paid more than they received in immediate assets. Usually does not come off the sheet.
