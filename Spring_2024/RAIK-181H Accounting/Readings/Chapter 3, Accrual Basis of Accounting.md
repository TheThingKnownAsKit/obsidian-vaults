# ACCRUAL BASIS OF ACCOUNTING
**Accrual basis of accounting** is a system where net income is reported during the period in which it is earned, not when it is collected/dispensed. Two principles guide measuring this:
* Revenue recognition principle
* Expense recognition (matching) principle

## Revenue Recognition Principle
The **revenue recognition principle** requires companies to recognize revenues in the period in which the company provides the goods/services, in the amount the company expects to receive.

Sales revenues are recognized during the period in which the goods/services are delivered in an amount equal to the sales price. If revenues is recognized before or after cash is received, then:
* *Cash received: WHEN EARNED*
	* Recognize the sales revenue at the same time they receive cash payment. Increases Cash and Sales Revenue
	![[Pasted image 20240204153745.png]]
* *Cash received: AFTER EARNED*
	* If the money is earned but not yet received, it is revenue after earned. Increase Accounts Receivable and increases Sales Revenue.
	* Later, when cash is received, increase Cash and reduce Accounts Receivable
	![[Pasted image 20240204154050.png]]
* Cash received: BEFORE EARNED
	* Example: Gift cards. The company receives the money at the time the card is purchased but have not yet earned the money because no goods/services have been redeemed
	* Upon receipt of the cash, record an increase in Cash and an increase in a liability account
	* When service/good is redeemed, increase Sales Revenue and decrease the liability account
	![[Pasted image 20240204154455.png]]

![[Pasted image 20240204154518.png]]

## Expense Recognition (Matching) Principle
The **Expense Recognition Principle** requires expenses to be recognized during the same period as the revenues they generate (also known as matching principle because expenses are "matched" with generated revenues)

Expenses are not "earned" until the resource bought is used

* *Cash paid: WHEN EXPENSE*
	* Example: Paying wages. The money is "earned" as an expense as the employee works
	* Decrease in Cash and an increase in Expenses (negative retained earnings)
	![[Pasted image 20240204154843.png]]
* *Cash paid: BEFORE EXPENSE*
	* Example: If supplies are bought, it is not technically an "earned" expense until the supplies is used. Therefore, it is an expense incurred before earned
	* Decrease Cash and increase whatever assets account. Record the expense on both sides
	![[Pasted image 20240204155151.png]]
* *Cash paid: AFTER EXPENSE*
	* Example: Utilities are typically billed after the resource has been used
	* Increase whatever liability Payable and increase expenses (retained earnings). Decrease cash and liabilities after paid
	![[Pasted image 20240204155353.png]]

## Types of Adjustments
There are four types of accounting adjustments made at the end of an accounting period:
![[Pasted image 20240204155659.png]]

The purpose of period-end adjustments are to assure that the Revenue Recognition Principle and Expense Recognition Principle are upheld

**Deferrals** are when cash is received/paid *before* the period in which the revenues/expenses are recognized
**Accruals** are when cash is received/paid *after* the period in which the revenues/expenses are recognized

![[Pasted image 20240204162212.png]]
![[Pasted image 20240204162226.png]]

### Recognizing Previously Deferred Revenue
**Deferred Revenues** are when cash is received from the sale of a service/product *before* they are delivered
	Increase Cash and increase a liability
		Usually unearned revenue (sometimes labeled as deferred revenue or advances from customers)
	Represents an obligation to perform a service or provide a product
If the service/good is provided, a period-end adjustment is necessary to recognize the revenue in that period
	Reduces Unearned Revenue and increases the related revenue account in the income statement

![[Pasted image 20240204160349.png]]

### Recognizing Previously Deferred Expenses
**Deferred expenses** arise when companies expend cash *before* the period in which the expense is recognized
	Reduce cash and increase an asset account
	End of period adjustment reduces the asset account and increases an expense

EXAMPLES:
* Office Supplies:

* Prepaid Rent:
	* Prepaid expenses increase, cash decrease Then:
	![[Pasted image 20240204160659.png]]
* Property, Plant, and Equipment:
	* Allocating the cost of buildings, equipment, and vehicles to the periods that benefit from their use is called **depreciation**. Because long-term assets help generate revenue for many years, a portion of their cost is expensed each accounting period. This is a *depreciation expense*.
	* There is no exact way to measure the amount the assets have benefitted each period, so depreciation expense is an estimate.
		* Method used in this example is: Acquisition Cost / Estimated useful life in years. This is **straight-line depreciation**
	* The asset account is not directly impacted. Instead, reduction is recorded in a contra account called **Accumulated Depreciation**.
	* An asset's **book value** represents the unexpired asset cost to be applied as an expense against future periods (Accumulated depreciation - related asset account)

**Contra accounts** are used to record reductions in, or offsets against, another account

### Recognizing Accrued Revenue
**Accrued revenues** arise when companies sell goods/services and cash is received *after* the period in which the related goods/services are delivered. Period-end adjustment is required to recognize revenues during the period in which they earned (when delivered)
The adjustment increases a receivable in the balance sheet and increases a revenue account in the income statement

Example: accrued fees
![[Pasted image 20240204161521.png]]

### Recognizing Accrued Expenses
**Accrued expenses** arise when cash paid for an expense occurs *after* the period in which the expense is incurred and recognized
Often regular expenses (weekly/monthly/etc). If the accounting period ends on a date that does not coincide with the cash payment, a period-end adjustment is required in order to recognize the expenses during the accounting period
	Increase a payable account and increase the related expense account in the income statement

Examples:
Accrued wages, accrued interest expense
	Because interest accumulates as time passes, an adjustment is needed during the end of the accounting period

# QUALITY OF ACCOUNTING NUMBERS
**Earnings quality** refers to the degree to which a company's financial statements fairly present its true underlying financial performance
Wall Street analyst often evaluate a company's quality of earnings by the degree to which these estimates are considered conservative or aggressive. Conservative lead to lower reported net income/net asset values. Conservative estimates (within reason) are generally considered to be a feature of high-quality financial reporting

# CLOSE TEMPORARY ACCOUNTS
**Permanent accounts** are the accounts presented on the balance sheet. *Assets, liabilities, and stockholders' equity accounts*. The balance in the account at the end of an accounting period is carried forward to the following accounting period

**Temporary accounts** are used to gather information for a particular accounting period. *Revenues, expenses, and dividend accounts* are temporary. These are transferred to retained earnings, which is a permanent account

The process of transferring the balances in temporary accounts to Retained Earnings is the **closing process/closing procedures**
