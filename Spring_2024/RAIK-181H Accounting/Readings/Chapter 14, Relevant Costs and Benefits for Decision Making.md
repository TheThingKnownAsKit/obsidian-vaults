# IDENTIFYING RELEVANT COSTS
A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">cost reduction proposal</mark> is a keep-or-replace decision with the desire to minimize cost of activities.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Outlay costs</mark> are costs that require future expenditures of cash or other resources. Outlay costs that differ under the decision alternatives are relevant; outlay costs that do not differ are irrelevant

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Opportunity cost</mark> is any benefit forgone as a result of rejecting one opportunity in favor of another opportunity

# DIFFERENTIAL ANALYSIS OF RELEVANT COSTS
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Differential cost analysis</mark> compares the relevant costs of each alternative to determine the best quantitative choice

A differential analysis is preferred to a complete analysis of all costs and revenues for a number of reasons:
* A differential analysis focuses on only those items that differ, providing a clearer picture of the impact of the decision. Management is less apt to be confused by this analysis than by one that combines relevant and irrelevant items. 
* A differential analysis contains fewer items, making it easier and quicker to prepare.
* A differential analysis can help to simplify complex situations (such as those encountered by multiple-product or multiple-plant firms), when it is difficult to develop complete firmwide statements to analyze all decision alternatives.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Joint products</mark> is two or more products simultaneously produced by a single process from a common set of inputs
The point in the process where the joint product becomes separately identifiable is called the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">split-off point</mark>. 
Materials and conversion costs incurred prior to the split-off point are called <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">joint costs</mark>. These are sunk costs because they are incurred prior to the decision point
