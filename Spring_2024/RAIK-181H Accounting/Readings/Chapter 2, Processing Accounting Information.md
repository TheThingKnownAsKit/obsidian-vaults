# ANALYZE TRANSACTIONS
Remember the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' equity

The **monetary unit concept** requires that a monetary unit is used to measure an entity's economic activity. Only items that can be expressed in monetary terms are recorded in financial statements

An **accounting transaction** is an economic event that must be recorded in the company's accounting records. Anything that affects the accounting equation

**Double-entry accounting** comes from the principle that there are usually two entries per transaction, therefore keeping the accounting equation in balance. Assets must equal liabilities + stockholders' equity

# THE "ACCOUNT" SYSTEM
The basic component of an accounting system is the **account**, which is an individual record of the increases and decreases in a specific asset, liability, or stockholders' equity item

## Source Documents
First step in analysis/recording is to identify evidence of a business transaction. Usually from a **source document**, printed forms or computer records that are generated when a firm engages in a business transaction
AT MINIMUM, include: dollar amount involved, date, names of transaction parties

Examples:
1. a supplier's invoice (evidence of purchase of supplies on account)
2. a bank check indicating the payment of an obligation
3. a deposit slip showing the amount of cash deposited in a bank
4. a cash receipt indicating cash received from a customer for services

## Chart of Accounts
A **chart of accounts** is a list of the titles of all accounts in a business's accounting system. Grouped by, and in the order of, the five major components of the expanded accounting equation:
1. assets
2. liabilities
3. stockholders' equity
4. revenues
5. expenses

## The General Ledger
A **general ledger** is a listing of each of a company's accounts and the amounts from all transactions recorded during the period. Ending balances are used in preparing financial statements
