# REASONS FOR BUDGETING
A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">budget</mark> is a formal plan of action expressed in monetary terms. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Operating budgets</mark> consist of the development of detailed plans to guide operations throughout the budget period. 

Proactive business structure instead of reactive

Budgets,
* Compel planning
* Promote communication and coordination
* Provide a guide to action and a basis of evaluation
* Aid in risk management

# GENERAL APPROACHES TO BUDGETING
Types of budgets include:
1. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Output/input approach</mark>
	1. Budgets physical inputs and costs as a function of planned unit-level activities. This approach is often used for service, merchandising, manufacturing, and distribution activities that have defined relationships between effort and accomplishment.
	2. For example, each unit produced requires 2 pounds of direct materials that cost $5 each, and the planned production volume is 25 units, the budgeted inputs and costs for direct materials are 50 pounds (25 units × 2 pounds per unit) and $250 (50 pounds × $5 per pound). 
2. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Activity based budget</mark>
	1. A type of output/input but based on activities you do to make the product than the raw materials. How many minutes will it take us to set up the machines? What are the setup costs? How many times do we go through quality control?
	2. Not very practical because it tries to quantify activity not resources
3. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Incremental budget</mark>
	1. You just get the budget from last year and then figure out the % change, increase or decrease, you will need for this year. You only need to figure out why you need, for example, 5% more this year. For when the relationship between outputs and inputs is weak or nonexistent
	2. Not as time consuming. Widely used in government and non-profit
4. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Minimum level budget</mark>
	1. You give everyone a minimum budget, then they have to argue for why they deserve more with a proposal. 
	2. Variant called 0-based budgeting where the minimum is 0 and you have to argue with for any budget at all

# MASTER BUDGET
The culmination of the budgeting process is the preparation of a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">master budget</mark> for the entire organization that considers all interrelationships among organization units. The master budget groups together all budgets and supporting schedules and coordinates all financial and operational activities, placing them into an organization-wide set of budgets for a given time period.

## Budgeted Financial Statements
The preparation of the master budget culminates in the preparation of budgeted financial statements. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Budgeted financial statements</mark> are pro forma statements that reflect the “as-if” effects of the budgeted activities on the actual financial position of the organization. That is, the statements reflect the results of operations assuming all budget predictions are correct.

# BUDGET DEVELOPMENT AND MANAGER BEHAVIOR
## Employee Participation
With a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">top-down or imposed budget</mark>, top management identifies the primary goals and objectives for the organization and communicates them to lower management levels. Because relatively few people are involved in top-down budgeting, an imposed budget saves time. It also minimizes the slack that managers at lower organizational levels are sometimes prone to build into their budgets. However, this nonparticipative approach to budgeting can have undesirable motivational consequences. Personnel who do not participate in budget preparation might lack a commitment to achieve their part of the budget. 

With a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">bottom-up or participative budget</mark>, managers at all levels—and in some cases, even 
nonmanagers—are involved in budget preparation. Budget proposals originate at the lowest level of management possible and are then integrated into the proposals for the next level, and so on, until the proposals reach the top level of management, which completes the budget. 
	Because they require the involvement of many people, the preparation period is longer than that for an imposed budget. Another disadvantage is the tendency of some managers to intentionally understate revenues or overstate expenses to provide <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">budgetary slack</mark>. This is a cushion created in a budget to minimize risk that managers might fail to meet budgeted results.

## Budgeting Periods
When a fixed time period is not particularly relevant to planning, an organization can use 
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">life-cycle budgeting</mark>, which involves developing a budget for a project’s entire life. An ice cream vendor at the beach might develop a budget for the season. A general contractor might budget costs for the entire (multiple-year) time required to construct a building. 

Under <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">continuous budgeting,</mark> the budget (sometimes called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">rolling budget</mark>) is based on 
a moving time frame. Such as the quarter system

## Open-Book Management
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Open book management</mark> involves sharing financial and related information with employees, teaching employees to understand financial numbers, encouraging employees to use the information in their work, and sharing financial results with employees, perhaps through a bonus program.
