# FRAUD
**Fraud** refers to any act by the management or employees of a business engaged in intentional deception for personal gain
Includes:
* Embezzlement
* Theft of assets
* Filing false insurance claims
* Filing false health claims
* Financial statement fraud

The **fraud triangle** are three elements always present when fraud occurs
1. A perceived pressure on the individual committing the fraud
	1. Financial need
	2. Management pressure
2. Some way to rationalize the fraudulent act
	1. "I am underpaid and the company owes me"
	2. "Everyone else is doing it"
	3. "I am only borrowing money and I will pay it back later"
3. A perceived opportunity

# INTERNAL CONTROL
## COSO Framework
The **Committee on Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO)** release a framework to help companies structure and evaluate their internal controls and reduce the risk of fraud of external threats

It identifies five internal control components:
1. The **control environment** sets the tone of the organization. If ethics and integrity are reinforced in work culture, it prevents rationalization
	1. Includes management philosophy and style; organizational structure and assignment of authority and responsibility; the process for attracting and developing competent employees; and the rewards to drive accountability for performance
2. **Risk assessment** involves identifying and analyzing risks
	1. External: economic conditions, industry competitors, regulation
	2. Internal: operating conditions
3. **Control activities** are the specific policies and procedures designed to reduce risk. Includes internal controls, prevention controls, and detection controls
	1. *Internal controls* are the measures undertaken to ensure the reliability of its accounting data, protect from theft or unauthorized use, ensure policies and procedures are being followed, and evaluate performance
	2. *Prevention control* deters a problem before it arises
		1. Establish clear lines of authority/responsibility
		2. *Segregate duties*
		3. Hire competent personnel
		4. provide physical and electronic controls (passwords, door locks, security cameras)
	3. *Detection control* discovers a program shortly after it arises
		1. Require mandatory vacations
		2. All business documents should have *control numbers* preprinted on them
		3. Develop plans and budgets
		4. Maintain adequate accounting records
4. **Information and communication** management must obtain or generate relevant and quality information
5. **Monitoring activities** involve ongoing evaluations, special evaluations, or some combination
	1. An *internal audit* provides independent appraisals of the company's financial statements, its internal controls, and its operations

When controls fail, that's what insurance is for kids

When two or more employees work together to circumvent or avoid prescribed internal controls, this is *employee collusion*

The **Sarbanes-Oxley Act** of 2002 (SOX) is the most far-reaching securities legislation since the SEC was created in 1934. It is an anti-fraud bill passed in the wake of high-profile accounting scandals (Enron and WorldCom) that destroyed hundreds of billions of dollars and resulted in some of the largest bankruptcies in US history
* Requires executive officers to sign a statement certifying financial statement accuracy (penalties 10-20 years)
* Section 404 requires public companies to hire external auditors to issue a formal opinion on the effectiveness of their internal controls
* Created the **Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB)**, a government agency tasked with overseeing the audits of public companies
	* Sets audit standards, inspects auditing practices, and disciplines

# ACCOUNTING FOR CASH
**Cash** includes coins, currency (paper money), checks, money orders, traveler's checks, and funds on deposit in a checkings/savings account
	Considered cash if 1. it is accepted by a bank or other financial institution (brokerage firm or credit union) for deposit, and 2. free from restrictions that would prevent its immediate use for paying debts

Near cash things that are not cash:
**Certificates of deposit (CDs)** are issued by a bank when cash is invested for a short period of time (3months-year). Pay fixed rate of interest
**Non-sufficient-funds check (NSF check)** check from an entity that had an insufficient cash balance in the bank when the holder of the check presented it to the bank for payment
IOU/Note receivable
	CDs are accounted as investments
	NSF checks and IOUs are Other Receivables

Different kinds of cash accounts;
* Cash in bank is any cash held in checkings/savings accounts
* Cash on hand includes cash items not yet deposited in bank
* Petty cash is cash on hand used for small disbursements and is maintained at the company's business location

**Cash and cash equivalents** are highly liquid, short-term investments of 90 days maturity or less in risk-free securities. Is combined into one cash account to match with statement of cash flows
