# PAR VALUE STOCK AND NO-PAR VALUE STOCK
**Par value** is a face value for capital stock specified by the corporate charter. No economic significance today because it's usually much less than the market price

Legal implications can be that it is the minimum amount that must be contributed for each share of stock. If issued at a discount, stockholder may have a liability for the amount of the discount should any creditor claims remain unsatisfied after company liquidation

Most states permit the issuance of capital stock without a par value, **no-par value stock**. In exchange, there is usually set **stated value** that will determine the legal capital. Stated value is treated similarly to par value in accounting

**Legal capital** is the minimum amount of contributed capital that must remain in a corporation as a margin of protection for creditors

# TYPES OF CAPITAL STOCK
The charter specifies a corporations maximum number of shares of each class of capital stock - **authorized shares**.

Shares actually held by stockholders are **outstanding shares**. The ones reacquired by a corporation are treasury stock

## Common Stock
When only one class of stock is issued, it is called **common stock**. Have the right to:
* vote on corporate matters (one vote per stock owned)
* to share in net income
* and to participate in additional issuances of stock
* and to share in asset distributions after paying creditors (bankruptcy)
* elect board of directors
* They have the **preemptive right** to maintain their proportion of ownership. If more shares are issued, the current owners receive the first opportunity to acquire, on a pro rata basis, the new shares
	* Can request to waive it

Classified common stock is common stock with tiers. Like Class A, Class B. Differ in their respective dividend rights or voting power. Usually happens when founders wish to acquire funds from the public while retaining voting control

## Preferred Stock
**Preferred stock** is a class of stock that has one or more preferences over common, usually
1. the payment of dividends
	1. Specified on stock contract as a percentage of par value or in dollars per share if there is no par value
	2. Usually **cumulative** - regular dividends accrued but not paid must be paid in addition to current year's dividends before any distribution to common stockholders. If it is **noncumulative**, they do not carry forward
2. the distribution of assets during liquidation
	1. Right to assets equal to the par value of their shares, or a larger value per share. Also includes any dividends in arrears

![[Pasted image 20240324125054.png]]
Arrears means dividends omitted in past years

Other:
1. Vote for board of directors (can be given by contract but not required, may be required by state though)
2. May be convertible into common stock at a specified conversion rate
3. A **participating** preferred stock shares any dividend distributions with common stock beyond the regular preferred dividend rate
4. If **callable** then the corporation can redeem the shares after a length of time at a price specified in stock contract. Similar to bond then

# STOCK ISSUANCES FOR CASH
If the corporation uses an investment bank to issue stock, they may **underwrite** a stock issue by agreeing to sell the shares on a firm commitment basis (buying the shares from the corporation and then reselling them). There is no risk in being unable to sell its stock

If unwilling to underwrite, a bank may sell as many shares as possible at a set price (best efforts basis)

Exchange stock for services or operating assets. Stock issuance is valued at the fair value of the assets or services received

# STOCK SPLITS
A **forward stock split** increases the number of shares outstanding and is accounted for by reducing the par value or stated value of the stock affected. Does not change overall equity balance
![[Pasted image 20240324125958.png]]

A reverse stock split is the opposite

# CASH DIVIDENDS AND STOCK DIVIDENDS
Usually paid in cash but can be paid in property or additional shares. Legally, declared dividends are an obligation of the firm, and an entry to record the dividend obligation is made on the dividend declaration date

![[Pasted image 20240324130717.png]]

Stock dividends are usually a percentage of retained earnings. For example, a 10% stock dividend, which is small, increases contributed capital and decreases retained earnings by 10% of the market value of the outstanding stock
