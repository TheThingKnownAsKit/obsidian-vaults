# RESPONSIBILITY ACCOUNTING
These performance reports should be prepared in accordance with the concept of 
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">responsibility accounting</mark>, which is the structuring of performance reports addressed to individual or group members of an organization to emphasize the factors they control.

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">cost center</mark> manager is only responsible for costs; there is no revenue responsibility. A cost center can be as small as a segment of a department or large enough to include a major aspect of the organization, such as all manufacturing activities.
![[Pasted image 20240422084032.png]]

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">revenue center</mark> manager is responsible for the generation of sales revenues. Even though the basic performance report of a revenue center emphasizes sales, revenue centers are likely to be assigned responsibility for the controllable costs they incur in generating revenues.

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">profit center</mark> manager is responsible for revenues, costs, and the resulting profits. A profit center could be an entire organization, but it is more frequently a segment of an organization such as a product line, marketing territory, or store.

An <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">investment center</mark> manager is responsible for the relationship between its profits and the total assets invested in the center. Investment center managers have a high degree of organization autonomy. In general, the management of an investment center is expected to earn a target profit per dollar invested.

# PERFORMANCE REPORTING FOR COST CENTERS
Financial performance reports for cost centers include a comparison of actual and budgeted (or allowed) costs and identify the difference as a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">variance</mark>. Can be favorable and unfavorable

A budget that is based on a prediction of sales and production is called a 
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">static budget</mark>. The operating budget explained in Chapter 16 is a static budget. Budgets can also be set for a series of possible production and sales volumes, or budgets can be adjusted to a particular level of production after the fact. These budgets, based on cost-volume relationships, are called <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">flexible budgets</mark>; they are used to determine what costs should be for a level of activity.

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">flexible budget variance</mark> is computed for each cost as the difference between the actual cost and the flexible budget cost.

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">standard cost</mark> is a measure of what it should cost to provide an activity or produce one unit of product under planned and efficient operating conditions.

# VARIANCE ANALYSIS FOR COSTS
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Standard cost variance analysis</mark> is a systematic approach to examining flexible budget variances. Actual costs are determined from the organization’s financial transactions. Flexible 
budget costs are determined by multiplying standard quantities allowed for the output times the standard price per unit.

# ESTABLISHING AND USING STANDARDS FOR DIRECT MATERIALS
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">materials price variance</mark> is the difference between the actual materials cost and the standard cost of actual materials inputs. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">materials quantity variance</mark> is the difference between the standard cost of actual materials inputs and the flexible budget cost for materials.
![[Pasted image 20240422092231.png]]

Materials price variance = AQ(AP − SP)
Materials quantity variance = SP(AQ − SQ)

# ESTABLISHING AND USING STANDARDS FOR DIRECT LABOR
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">labor rate variance</mark> is the difference between the actual cost and the 
standard cost of actual labor inputs. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">labor efficiency variance</mark> is the difference between the standard cost of actual inputs and the flexible budget cost for labor. 

![[Pasted image 20240422101947.png]]

Labor rate variance = AH(AR − SR)
Labor efficiency variance = SR(AH − SH)

# ESTABLISHING AND USING STANDARDS FOR VARIABLE OVERHEAD
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">variable overhead spending variance</mark> is the difference between the actual variable overhead cost and the standard variable overhead cost for the actual inputs of the measure. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">variable overhead efficiency variance</mark> is the difference between the standard variable overhead cost for the actual inputs of the measure and the flexible budget cost allowed for variable overhead based on outputs.

![[Pasted image 20240422102201.png]]

Standard cost of actual inputs = (AQ × SR)
Flexible budget cost = (SQ × SR)
Variable overhead efficiency variance = SR(AQ − SQ)

## Fixed Costs Variance
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">fixed overhead budget variance</mark> is, simply, the difference between budgeted and actual fixed overhead.

Fixed overhead budget variance = Actual fixed overhead − Budgeted fixed overhead

# PERFORMANCE REPORTS FOR REVENUE CENTERS
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">revenue variance</mark> is the difference between the budgeted sales volume at the budgeted selling price and the actual sales volume at the actual selling price.
Revenue variance = (Actual volume × Actual price) − (Budgeted volume × Budgeted price)

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">sales price variance</mark> is computed as the change in selling price times the actual sales volume.
Sales price variance = (Actual selling price − Budgeted selling price) × Actual sales volume

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">sales volume variance</mark> indicates the impact of the change in sales volume on revenues, assuming there was no change in selling price. The sales volume variance is computed as the difference between the actual and the budgeted sales volumes times the budgeted selling price.
Sales volume variance = (Actual sales volume − Budgeted sales volume) × Budgeted selling price

## Controllable Costs
The controllable costs of revenue centers include variable and fixed selling costs. These costs are sometimes further classified into order-getting and order-filling costs. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Order-getting costs</mark> are incurred to obtain customers’ orders (for example, advertising, salespersons’ salaries and commissions, travel, telephone, and entertainment). <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Order-filling costs</mark> are distribution costs incurred to place finished goods in the hands of purchasers (for example, storing, packaging, and transportation).

## As Profit Centers
To evaluate the sales department as a profit center, the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">net sales volume variance</mark> must be 
computed. The net sales volume variance indicates the impact of a change in sales volume on the contribution margin given the budgeted selling price and the standard variable costs.

Net sales volume variance = (Actual volume − Budgeted volume) × Budgeted contribution margin
