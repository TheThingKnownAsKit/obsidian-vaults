# MISC INFO
Accounting information designed for external users is **financial accounting.** Internal issues is **managerial accounting.**

## Ethics and Accounting
Regulatory organizations/thing (help):
* **American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA)**
	* Professional code of ethics to guide the conduct of CPA accountants
* **Institute of Management Accountants (IMA)**
	* Written standard of ethical conduct for accountants employed in the private sector
* **Sarbanes-Oxley Act**
	* Passed in 2002 after Enron/WorldCom to improve trust in accounting. Company's top management must certify in writing that the financial statements are "fairly presented" and are liable for prosecution for fraudulent certification. Must also report the effectiveness of internal controls to prevent/detect errors in financial reporting
	* Also known as SOX act
* **Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)**
	* Standards, procedures, and rules accounts follow when preparing financial statements
* **Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB)**
	* Nongovernmental entity consisting of a seven-member board. Takes input to consider new or changed accounting principles. New additions require majority vote
* **U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)**
	* Federal agency focused on regulating the interstate sale of stocks and bonds
	* Can technically set standards but largely lets the FASB do that
* **Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB)**
	* Established by Sox Act and creates auditing standards, known as **generally accepted auditing standards (GAAS)**
	* Monitors quality of financial statement audits
* **International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)**
	* Sets international accounting standards, known as **International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)**
	* More than 140 nations/jurisdictions require or permit the use of IFRS

# FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
## Balance Sheet
Reflects a framework called the **accounting equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity
![[Pasted image 20240128204122.png]]
**Assets** are economic resources own by the business
**Liabilities** are obligations or debt that must be paid in cash/goods/services at some point because of past transactions
**Stockholders' equity** refers to the ownership (stockholder) claims on the assets of the business. Represents a residual claim on assets aka the assets remaining after all liabilities have been satisfied. That is why this is sometimes called **net assets**. **Stockholders' equity = Assets - Liabilities**

![[Pasted image 20240128204615.png]]

## Income Statement
The **income statement** (also known as statement of operations, profit and loss statement or P&L, the statement of income, and the earnings statement) reports the results of operations for a business for a given time period (usually a quarter or year)
Lists revenues and expenses

**Sales revenues** (revenue, sales, net sales, net revenue) are increases to a company's resources or decrease to its obligations when pending goods/services are provided to customers
Measured by the value of the assets received in exchange for the goods or services delivered

**Net income** (net earnings or net profit) is total revenue - total expenses. **Net loss** if the expenses exceed income
![[Pasted image 20240128205021.png]]

## Statement of Stockholders' Equity
Reports the events causing an increase or decrease in a business's stockholders' equity during a given time period, including both the changes in a company's common stock and changes in its retained earnings
Consists of two parts: contributed capital and earned capital

**Contributed capital** is a measure of the capital contributed by the stockholders of a company when they purchase shares (common shares/stock)
	A portion of the amount paid for the shares may be allocated to additional paid-in capital
**Earned capital** is a measure of the capital that is earned by a company, reinvested in the business, and not distributed to its stockholders (retained earnings)

## Relations Among the Financial Statements
![[Pasted image 20240128205807.png]]
A. The company's net income (or net loss) for a period is an input to the statement of stockholders' equity
B. The ending common stock, retained earnings, and total equity are inputs to the balance sheet
C. The statement of cash flows explains the change in the cash balance on the balance sheet for a period

Income statement, statement of stockholders' equity, and statement of cash flows are **period-of-time statements**.
Balance sheet is a **point-in-time statement**.

# ADDITIONAL INFORMATION
The **Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)** contains management's interpretation of the company's recent performance and financial condition
Might also discuss future opportunities/risks (forward-looking)

**Notes to the financial statements** provide more information regarding how the company determined the value of its inventory, a detailed chart to explain the PP&E account, description of pending lawsuits, etc

**Auditor's report** describes activities undertaken by the company's independent auditor and reports their opinion regarding the validity of the financial statements and financial health
