# LONG-RANGE PLANNING AND CAPITAL BUDGETING
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Capital expenditures</mark> are investments of financial resources in projects to develop or introduce new products or services, to expand current production or service capacity, or to change current production or service facilities

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Capital budgeting</mark> is a process that involves identifying potentially desirable projects for capital expenditures, evaluating expenditure proposals, and selecting proposals that meet minimum criteria

# CAPITAL BUDGETING MODELS THAT CONSIDER TIME VALUE OF MONEY
They consider the time value of money, such as net present value models and internal rate of return models

![[Pasted image 20240428165834.png]]
![[Pasted image 20240428165926.png]]

A project's <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">net present value</mark>, usually computed as of the time of the initial investment, is the present value of the project's net cash inflows from operations and disinvestment less the amount of the initial investment

When computing a project's net present value, the cash flows occurring at different points in time are adjusted for the time value of money using a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">discount rate</mark> that is the minimum rate of return required for the project to be acceptable

![[Pasted image 20240428170110.png]]

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">internal rate of return (IRR)</mark> is the discount rate that equates the present value of a project's cash inflows with the present value of the project's cash outflows. Other ways to describe IRR include 1. the minimum rate that could be paid for the money invested in a  without losing money, and 2. the discount rate that results in a project's net present value equaling zero
![[Pasted image 20240428170246.png]]

When discounting models are used, an organization's cost of capital is often used as this discount rate. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">cost of capital</mark> is the average cost an organization pays to obtain the resources necessary to make investments. This average considers:
* Effective interest rate on debt (notes or bonds)
* Effective dividend rate on preferred stock
* Discount rate that equates the present value of all dividends expected on common stock over the life of the organization to the current market value of the organization's common stock
Thus, specifically for equity, it is:
$$
CostOfEquityCapital=\frac{CurrentAnnualDividendPerCommonShare}{CurrentMarketPricePerCommonShare}+ExpectedDividendGrowthRate
$$
