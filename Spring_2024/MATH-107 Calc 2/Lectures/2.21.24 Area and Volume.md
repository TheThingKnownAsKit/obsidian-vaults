## Right Triangle Vertical Slice
Right triangle with side lengths 3 and 6 inches. Slice of height h. x is the length of the first section of triangle where $0\le x \le 6$

Area of little rectangle = $\Delta x*h$
Put in terms of x
$\frac{h}{3}=\frac{6-x}{6}$ by similar triangles
$h=\frac{1}{2}(6-x)$
Area of little rectangle = $\frac{1}{2}(6-x)\Delta x$
If you make a lot of little rectangles then add them then it's an approximation of the whole triangle

$A=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}(6-x)\Delta x$
Oh joy, it's Riemann sum's again
So, $\int_{0}^{6}\frac{1}{2}(6-x)dx$
Take the limit and $\Delta x\to 0$, we get $A=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{6}(6-x)dx$

$\frac{1}{2}[6x-\frac{x^2}{2}]\Bigr|_{0}^{6}$
$=4$

## Circle Horizontal Slice
Find the area of the circle with radius 3
The distance from the center of the circle to the slice is h, and the width (up and down) of the slice is $\Delta h$

Area of the little slice is $x\Delta h$ where x is the length from the radius to the edge of the slice (half the total length)

h, center, radius, and where h meets the edge form a right triangle
$A=(2\sqrt{9-h^{2}})\Delta h$
Where $-3\le h \le 3$ (all the way up and down)

Area of the whole circle is
$A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\sqrt{9-h^{2}}\Delta h$
Riemann sum
Take the limit
$A=\int_{-3}^{3}2\sqrt{9-h^2}dh$

Trig sub
$h=3\sin\theta$
$dh=3\cos\theta d\theta$
When h = -3. $\theta=\frac{-\pi}{2}$
When h = 3, $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}$

$A=2\int_{\frac{-\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(3\cos\theta)(3\cos\theta)d\theta$
$=18\int_{\frac{-\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{2}\theta d\theta$
Use trig identity
$18[(\frac{1}{2}\theta+\frac{\sin2\theta}{4})\Bigr|_{\frac{-\pi}{2}}^{\frac{-\pi}{2}}]=9\pi$
