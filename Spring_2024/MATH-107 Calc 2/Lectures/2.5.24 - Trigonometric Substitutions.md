Used when you have something in the form $\sqrt{a^2\pm x^2}$ (usually in the denominator)
Use sine in the - case and tan in the + case

Basically you put values on a triangle and substitute a trig function in for the values
($\sqrt{25-x^2}$ is $5\cos x$ on a triangle, so substitute the cos in). Doesn't really matter what values you put on what sides, but it will affect if you're doing a cos or sin substitution

Double angle formula:
$\sin^2x=\frac{1-\cos2x}{2}$
$\cos^2x=\frac{1+\cos2x}{2}$
Reminders:
$\sin(2\theta)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$

## Example: Sin
Find $\int\sqrt{25-x^2}dx$
Use triangle to determine what is what
The sqrt is part of the Pythagorean Theorem

Pythagorean u-sub
$u=2\theta$
$du=2d\theta$
$d\theta=\frac{du}{2}$
$\int\frac{\cos u}{2}\frac{du}{2}=\frac{1}{4}\int\cos u$


$\sin\theta=\frac{x}{5}$ so $x=5\sin\theta$
$dx=5\cos\theta d\theta$
Plug it into the original equation
$\int\sqrt{25-x^2}dx$
$=\int(5\cos\theta)(5\cos\theta)d\theta$
$=25\int\cos^2\theta d\theta$
Remember $\cos^{2}\theta=\frac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2}$
$25\int[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\cos 2\theta}{2}]d\theta$
$25[\frac{1}{2}\theta+\frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}]+C$

Need to put back into terms of x
Since $\sin\theta=\frac{x}{5}$, $\theta=\arcsin\frac{x}{5}$
$=25[\frac{1}{2}\arcsin(\frac{x}{5})+\frac{2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{4}]+C$
$=25[\frac{1}{2}\arcsin(\frac{x}{5})+\frac{(\frac{x}{5})\sqrt{\frac{25-x^2}{5}}}{2}]+C$
$=25[\frac{1}{2}\arcsin(\frac{x}{5})+\frac{x\sqrt{25-x^2}}{50}]+C$

## Example: Tan
Find $\int\frac{x^2}{(x^2+9)^{\frac{3}{2}}}dx$
Triangle time. x on up side, $\theta$ on bottom corner, 3 as bottom side. Pythagorean Theorem the hypotenuse to $\sqrt{9+x^2}$
$\tan\theta=\frac{x}{3}$, so $x=3\tan\theta$
$dx=3\sec^{2}\theta d\theta$

Substitute in
$=\int(\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+4}})^3dx$
$=\int(\sin^3\theta)(3\sec^{2}\theta)d\theta$
Get in terms of just sin/cos
$=3\int\frac{\sin^3\theta}{\cos^2\theta}d\theta$
$=3\int\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}\sin\theta d\theta$
$=3\int\frac{1-\cos^{2}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta}\sin\theta d\theta$

Use u-substitution
$u=\cos\theta$
$du=-\sin\theta d\theta$

Plug into equation
$=3\int\frac{1-u^2}{u^2}(-du)$
$=-3\int\frac{1-u^2}{u^2}du$
$=-3\int[u^{-2}-1]du$
$=-3[\frac{u^{-1}}{-1}-u]+C$
$=3[u^{-1}+u]+C$
$=3[\frac{1}{\cos\theta}+\cos\theta]+C$
Consult the triangle:tm:
$=3[\frac{\sqrt{9+x^2}}{3}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{9+x^2}}]+C$
