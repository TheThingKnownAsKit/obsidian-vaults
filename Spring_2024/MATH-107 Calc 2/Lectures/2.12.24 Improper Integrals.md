Two types:
1. One or both limits of the integration is -$\infty$, $\infty$
2.  The limits of integration are numbers but the integrand becomes infinity. eg $\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{x^2}dx$. There's an asymptote

IF INFINITY IN LOWER BOUND:
	If the limit exists, we say it **converges** if f(x) is nonnegative for $x\ge a$
	If the limit does not exist, say the integral **diverts** if f(x) is nonnegative for $x\le a$
		Is x greater than or less than the lower bound as the function increases?
IF INFINITY IN UPPER BOUND:
	It converges if f(x) is nonnegative for $x \le b$
	Diverges if f(x) is nonnegative for $x\ge b$
		Is x less than or greater than the upper bound as the function increases?

## Infinity in Bounds
![[Pasted image 20240212105951.png]]
Example:
$\int_0^{\infty}e^{-x}dx$
$=\lim_{b\to \infty}\int_{0}^{b}e^{-x}dx$
$=e-e^{-b}$ then take the limit
$\lim_{b\to \infty}(1-e^{-b})$ as b increases it gets closer and closer to 0, so this = 1. It converges

Example:
$\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^2}dx$
$\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{-1}{b}+1$
As b increases, the reciprocal gets closer and closer to 0. Therefore, it = 1 and converges

Converges if P > 1 and diverges if P $\le$ 1

## Infinity in Integrand
![[Pasted image 20240212110158.png]]
![[Pasted image 20240212110212.png]]

Example:
$\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}dx$
The problem here is that you can't divide by 0, so the lower bound makes the equation invalid with a hole in the graph

$\lim{b\to0^+}\int_{b}^{1}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}dx$
$\lim{b\to0^+}2[1-\sqrt{b}]=2$
So, it converges and it = 2
