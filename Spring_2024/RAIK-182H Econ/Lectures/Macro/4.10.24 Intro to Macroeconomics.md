# MACRO INTRO
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Macroeconomics</mark> is the study of economy as a whole. Big picture patterns and trends

Microeconomics focuses on supply and demand graphs of specific markets
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Macro focuses on TOTAL INCOME, OUTPUT, etc. It is aggregating ALL the markets</mark>

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Income per capita</mark> is the amount of income per person on average

Macroeconomics is interested in the causes of <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">recessions</mark> (an economic downturn).
	More specifically, a period of 2 or more quarters when aggregate economic output has fallen

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">unemployment rate</mark> is the number of people ACTIVELY LOOKING for a job for at least 4 weeks while not having a job. AND you are available for work
Unemployment conditions:
1. Actively looking for jobs
2. Not currently employed
3. 1 and 2 for at least 4 weeks
4. Available for work
The math is $URate=\frac{U}{U+E}$ where U is unemployed people and E is employed people

# GDP
Key concept of macro is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">gross domestic product (GDP)</mark> which is the market value of every good or service produced in in a specific territory during a time period (usually quarters).
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Notes on what is included:</mark>
* NOTE: THIS ONLY INCLUDES FINAL GOODS AND SERVICES
* Additionally, since it is a measure of production you also don't include the resale of goods (unless you make improvements)
* Does not include things purchased or produced abroad

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">GDP measures three things:</mark>
1. Production: How much stuff is produced?
2. Income: How much income do households receive?
3. Expenditure: How much did people spend?
All these values are identical so you can use any of the three to calculate GDP

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">GDP misses:</mark>
* depreciation of assets or deductions of any kind really
* It also misses the informal or underground economy
* It does not include home production (things you produce yourself for yourself or other people for free)
* Externalities
* Leisure
* Overseas production (if a firm from a country has global branches)
* Happiness

## Production
For GDP, production is measured in <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">value added</mark>. This is the firm's sales revenue minus purchases of intermediate products. This is calculated AT EACH STAGE OF PRODUCTION

For example, a US lumber company sells 400 of lumber to a factory, which sells a 1,000 sofa, which sells at 1,500 to a family
Value added for lumber company = 400
Value added for factory = 1000-400 = 600
Value added for sofa company = 1500 - 1000 = 500
Add them all up for total value added = 1,500
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">NOTICE HOW IT'S THE SAME AS FINAL PRICE (this only counts if everything is produced and sold in the same territory, otherwise it'll be difference) </mark>

Suppose the lumber came from canada, get rid of the beginning 400
Total value added is only 1,100 then

## Income
You can measure GDP by tracking income. There are two categories:
1. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Labor Income</mark>: wages, benefits, etc. Monetary value you get from working
2. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Capital Income</mark>: Payments to owners of factories, shareholders, landlords, etc. Giving money to someone else

Most people receive both types. 

The equation would be:
$$
GDP=LaborIncome+CapitalIncome
$$

Also, remember aggregate income = production = expenditure

## Expenditure
You can also calculate GDP using expenditure

There are five main components:
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Consumption</mark>: Market value of consumption goods, services
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Investment</mark>: Value of new physical capital
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Government</mark>: Spending on things (tanks, bridges)
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Exports</mark>: Goods produced here but sold abroad
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Imports</mark>: Goods produced abroad but purchased here (subtract)

The formula is:
$$
Y=C+I+G+(EX-IM)
$$
