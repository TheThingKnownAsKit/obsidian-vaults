<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Per-capita GDP helps adjust for population differences when comparing countries. GDP per persona on average.</mark>
Because it is an average, this number is typically inaccurate and overinflated (depends on the wealth gaps). Without it, 100 people can produce more than 10, so normal GDP is not comparable

## Purchase Power Parity
There's multiple approaches to converting GDP when dealing with countries who use different currencies:
1. Naive approach: Convert to currency using the exchange rates (bad one)
	1. Problem: things cost different prices in every country. Willingness to pay and productions costs will vary depending on country conditions
	2. For example, a burger is 5 bucks in the US but only 2 in South Africa, both in USD
2. What you should actually do is use <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">purchasing power parity</mark>. It adjusts figures for differences in the price of a "bundle" of goods. It wants to know if you gave two people from different countries the same amount of some currency, how much could they buy? How much would it cost both people to buy the same "bundle" of goods?

## Measuring Income and Factors of Inequality

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Differences in income can come from three sources:</mark>
1. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Human capital</mark>: Each person's stock of skills to produce output or economic value
2. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Physical capital</mark>: Machines, buildings, and other goods used for production
3. <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Technology</mark>: The knowledge or organization system used to combine labor and capital. Application of human and physical capital as well as knowledge
	1. This is typically treated as the leftovers from human and physical. What can we not explain with the other two?

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">aggregate production function</mark> is:
$$
Y=A*F(K,h)
$$
Where:
* Y is GDP
* F is a function of capital and labor
	* K is capital
	* H is efficiency units (H = L x h where L is labor and h is human capital)
* A is technology

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">This function reveals some things that can cause income inequality:</mark>
* Total labor: A larger share of working means more GDP in the equation. Note, NOT total population. It is the total number of people who can work
* Human capital: Better educated or more skilled workforce -> more GDP
* Capital stock and land: An economy with a more physical capital can have more productive workers (working with a hoe vs a tractor)
* <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Technology: This is one of the most important factors in income inequality.</mark> The knowledge to produce certain goods and services combined with the efficiency of a population has a massive impact on income. ## Measuring Poverty
You need to define poverty to measure it.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Absolute poverty</mark> is measuring poverty by using the poverty line. For example, if you need 10 dollars a day to survive, the poverty line is 10 dollars and anyone below it is in poverty. This is much more measurable and common in economic discussions
	Country specific, each one has it's own line
	The World Bank defines the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">international poverty line</mark> as 2.15 per day (as of 2017). In US, the line is 24.55 per day
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Relative poverty</mark> is more subjective to opinions and variances.
