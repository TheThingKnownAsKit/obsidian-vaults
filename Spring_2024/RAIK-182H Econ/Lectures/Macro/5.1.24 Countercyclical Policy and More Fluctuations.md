# COUNTERCYCLICAL POLICY
Recessions are bad so let's fix it

Government tools to smooth out the business cycle in an attempt to get rid of or lessen recessions are <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">countercyclical policies</mark>
	<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Countercyclical monetary policy</mark> is when the Fed (central bank) alters bank reserves, interest rates, etc
	<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Countercyclical fiscal policy</mark> is when legislative and executive branches alter taxes and government expenditures
	<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Contractory monetary policy</mark> is just correcting the market? Overcorrecting

## Monetary Policy
* <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Open market operations</mark> is when the Fed buys and sells government bonds from private banks
	* Sell bonds to diversify bank investment portfolios
	* Buys bonds if they want the bank to have more liquid cash supply in exchange for a long-term asset
		* Supply of credit increases, interest rates fall, investment increases

![[Pasted image 20240501131249.png]]
The Fed buys bonds which decreases interest rates -> banks have more supply of credit so they give more credit out and decrease their interest rates -> VMPL rises which makes labor demand increase

Basically if recessions happen, the Fed will increase their reserve



Another tool is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Reserve requirements</mark> is when the Fed can alter required quantity that banks are required to have on hand. The money they have liquid and not invested
* Lower requiresments -> less demand for reserves -> lower Fed reserve rates

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Interest rate on deposits</mark> sets the rate on reserves deposited at Fed
* Sets a floor on fed funds rate by raising opportunity cost of lending. If the Fed has a lower interest rate than your bank, why would you bother getting a loan anywhere else?
* Increasingly important tool after recent expansions of M2

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Discount window</mark> is when the Fed also lends reserves directly to banks at a higher rate. Typically done if a bank is in desperate need for liquidity on short notice and doesn't want to ask other banks. The Fed is a lender of last resort. Effectively puts a ceiling on the federal funds rate
![[Pasted image 20240501132056.png]]

When the Fed's nominal interest rate is 0, we have hit the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">zero lower bound</mark> which means the nominal interest rates cant be effectively set below 0
	Savers would rather put their money away than pay the bank to store it
	Reduces effectiveness of Fed's conventional policies
To help with this, the Fed can use <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">quantitive easing</mark> which is when they buy long-term bonds. Everything before these were short-run treasury bills
	Increases supply of long-term loans, lowers long-term interest rates
	Trying to reduce the amount of people who hold long-term bonds and increase short-term debt

The Fed also tries to manage economic expectations because if people expect high inflation then when they adjust prices accordingly they've accidentally caused inflation. The Fed tries to alter expected real interest rates with <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">forward guidance</mark>, which is essentially a promise to pursue a particular monetary policy
