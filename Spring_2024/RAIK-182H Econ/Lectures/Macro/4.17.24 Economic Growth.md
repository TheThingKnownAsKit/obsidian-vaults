# DEFINING ECONOMIC GROWTH
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Economic growth</mark> is the increase in real GDP per capita of an economy. It is the percentage change in GDP per capita.
$$
Growth_{t,t+1}=\frac{rGDP_{t+1}-rGDP_t}{rGDP_t}
$$

A lot of economies exhibit <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">exponential growth</mark> where the growth RATE is constant. If the growth rate is 2%, then 2% of 100 is more than that of 50. Put a graph in log form to see a more accurate one representation of economic growth instead of exponential (huge spikes even though the RATE is constant)

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Rule of 70</mark> means that you can find how long it takes per-capita GDP to double by,
$$
TimeToDouble=\frac{70}{GrowthRate}
$$

To determine what drives economic growth, let's look at the aggregate production function
$Y=A*F(K,H)$
Where:
* Y is GDP
* F is a function of capital and labor
	* K is capital
	* H is efficiency units (H = L x h where L is labor and h is human capital)
* A is technology
This is discussed below. These things are <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">proximate causes</mark> where they certainly influence things but don't appear to be the fundamental cause

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">The real fundamental drivers of economic growth appears (but are not confirmed) to be things like:</mark>
* Geography hypothesis: climate, topography, access to water (coasts and major rivers), etc
* Culture hypothesis: Cultural values are the key
* Institutions hypothesis: Formal and informal rules governing society are the key

## H. Role of Labor in Growth
This section is about H = L x h where L is labor and h is human capital

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Growth in population does NOT necessarily cause an increase in GDP/capita. What matters is the labor force participation rate.</mark> This is the L

Beyond raw numbers, the human capital of the labor force also matters. These are things like primary education and secondary education. The <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">skills and education</mark> of your work force. This is the h

This is limited by the consequences of overpopulation, a lot of retirees, and the quality of the education given

## K. Physical Capital
The source of physical capital is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">investment</mark>. Investment is basically equivalent to savings. It is holding off consumption. The less you consume the more you can invest
$$
I=Y-C=S
$$
Where I is investment, Y is GDP, C is consumption, and S is savings

Remember the law of diminishing returns. Also remember that physical capital depreciates. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">The result of these two facts is that eventually, the cost of more investment exceeds the benefit of consumption</mark>
This means that long-run growth is not sustainable through just capital accumulation

## A. Technology
We have discovered that labor and physical capital have their limits as drivers of growth. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Technology is NOT SUBJECT TO DIMINISHING RETURNS</mark>. It also makes physical and human capital more valuable, incentivizing investing

Technology is worth a lot just by itself but not only that, it makes labor and physical capital more valuable than they would be otherwise. This is why it's so important

Ideas can be shared and do not depreciation. They also inspire more ideas. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">There are potentially no limits to economic growth and rising living standards when driven by technology</mark>
