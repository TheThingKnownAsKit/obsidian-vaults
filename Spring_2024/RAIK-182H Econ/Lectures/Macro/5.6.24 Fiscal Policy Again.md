# FISCAL POLICY
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Fiscal policy</mark> uses government expenditures to try and monitor and control the economy. It can be contractionary or expansionary. Controlled by the President and Congress

Some fiscal policies are <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">automatic stabilizers</mark> which are components of the government budget that automatically adjust to smooth out fluctuations.
	Unemployment benefits
	SNAP benefits
	Taxes (designed to collect less during a downturn)

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Discretionary fiscal policy</mark> is the opposite of automatic. They have to actively do this. Generally consists of extra tax cuts/increases, new expenditures, etc

Negative shock:
	Government can increase spending to increase jobs available

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">government expenditure multiplier</mark> is how much multiplication the government gets from expenditures. How much does it increase the demand curve by spending this money?
	Expenditures can also crowd out the labor market if it starts COMPETING with privately funded projects. This is meant to decrease unemployment not drive up wages

They may impose a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">contractionary</mark> fiscal policy by raising taxes and cutting spending if the wages are being driven too high

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">ALL AFFECT DEMAND CURVE</mark>. Expenditure increases demand 

Arguments against:
Lag - takes forever to do absolutely anything
Leads to wasteful spending
