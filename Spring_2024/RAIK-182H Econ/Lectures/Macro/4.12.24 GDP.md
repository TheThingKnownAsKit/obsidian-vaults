# CALCULATING GDP AND GDP GROWTH
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">GDP is mostly used to measure the size and growth of an economy</mark>. It gives a sense of how much "stuff" an economy is producing

Complications in calculating it arise when you realize that it is measured with dollars, and the value of the dollar changes not only from currency to currency but also from inflation and such. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">We don't want to confuse changes in PRICE with changes in the amount of STUFF PRODUCED</mark>. You can distinguish this with real GDP vs nominal GDP

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Real GDP</mark> is the total value of production using market prices from a base year. You hold prices constant and just change the number of stuff produced
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Nominal GDP</mark> is the total value of production using current market prices

To calculate <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">GDP Growth/Shrink</mark>, 
$$
GDPGrowth=\frac{GDP_{Year2}-GDP_{Year1}}{GDP_{Year1}}
$$
Example with the above: $\frac{250K-200K}{200K}=0.25\to25\%$ growth

# INFLATION
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Inflation</mark> is the OVERALL increase in the level of prices. Not individual brand prices, but all prices of something in a market. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">inflation rate</mark> is how much it increased since a given time period as a %

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">index</mark> is I do not know but we need it to measure inflation. A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">GDP deflactor</mark> tells us how much change have we seen in the price of goods and services produced in the country.
It can be calculated using the below where nGDP is nominal and rGDP is real:
$$
GDPDeflector=\frac{nGDP}{rGDP}*100
$$
Example using the example from the previous section, $\frac{250K}{214K}*100=117\to17\%$ inflation



The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Consumer Price Index (CPI)</mark> is the change in price of goods found in a common consumer household. What is the inflation of goods the average consumer buys on the regular?
It is calculated the same as GDP except you only include appropriate products
