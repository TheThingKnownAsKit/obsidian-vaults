# CREDIT MARKET
Credit is like any other good. To get it you have to pay something, the <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">thing you're paying is just the interest rate</mark>

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">nominal interest rate</mark> is a constant defined interest rate for a time period.
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">relevant cost</mark> is the real rate accounting for inflation over time: nominal interest minus inflation. This is the Fisher Equation

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Fisher Equation</mark> is used to calculate relevant interest rate
$$
r=i-\pi
$$
Where r is real interest rate, i is the nominal interest rate, and $\pi$ is the inflation rate

The <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">return for the borrower can be calculated using</mark>:
$$
Return\% = \frac{R-C}{C}
$$
Where c is the cost of investment and R is the revenue generated by the investment



The market for loanable funds or the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">credit market</mark>. Pretend there's only one kind of loan. Real interest rate is on the y-axis and quantity is on the x-axis.

The willingness for someone to take out credit at a certain price, only the interest rate changing, is the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">credit demand curve</mark> that slopes downwards because higher interest rate -> lower profits
Primary source of credit is savers. In return for giving credit, you receive a return of <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">1 + r in real interest</mark>. Higher r incentives saving which is the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">credit supply curve</mark>.

Credit demand shifters:
* Changes in perceived business opportunities
* Changes in household preferences or expectations
* Changes in government policy
Credit supply shifters:
* Changes in the savings motives of households
* Changes in the savings motives of firms

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Interest ceilings work the same way as price ceilings</mark>. Cause DWL and shortage and all that.

---

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Credit markets match borrowers and lenders</mark> for answering who do you ask for money / who do you give money to. Savers get a return and borrowers get a good rate

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">This models limitations are not the fact that:</mark>
* Everyone receives the same interest rate
* No defaulting
* No information problems, discrimination, or access differences to lenders
* Predatory loaning practices
* Different types of loans
* Risk factors making rates vary
