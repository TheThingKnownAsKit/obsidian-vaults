Why do we trade?
![[Pasted image 20240216124321.png]]

The opportunity cost of each production possibility curve is the slope of it. For example, the slope of Jeremy's line is -20, so 20 points per dataset

Because the economist's opportunity cost is lower, we say they have a **comparative advantage** in datasets. It is about reallocating resources to where they're most productive. Do more of what you're good at and less of the other things
Things that can determine comparative advantage:
* Natural resources
* Stocks of man-made resources
* Technology
* Characteristic of workforce
* Relative abundance of capital and labor
* Climate

![[Pasted image 20240216124915.png]]
If each focused on what they're good at, they're possible combinations of goods increases dramatically. Their **production possibility curves** combine and move. If they both **specialize** they can achieve combinations not possible by themselves. This is why trade is important

Costs of trade: shipping, language barriers, laws

Even if someone has **absolute advantage** (equal production in both goods) in something, still benefits from trade due to combined work
Even if someone has higher production, the other person can have a lower opportunity cost and still benefit. The economist has a lower opportunity cost than Jeremy even though he's better at everything

The opportunity cost for each individual will determine their **terms of trade**. This is the range in which their reservation value makes both of it worth their while. The actual trade price is not something easily predicted, hence the range
