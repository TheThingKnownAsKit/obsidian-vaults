# SUPPLY AND DEMAND
## Buyer Behavior
In a decision-making buying process, consider:
* The *price* of an item
* The *quantity demand* of an item

This can be modeled using a **demand schedule**
Side by side column chart

It can also be modeled using a **demand curve** which is a normal graph with a vertical and horizontal axis. Usually downward sloping \ going downwards
* On a demand curve: Price for y quantity for x 
* Example of *Ceteris paribus*: "holding all else equal". Basically keeping all other factors constant to focus on the effect of ONLY changing the price 
* Models willingness to pay. Models **diminishing marginal benefit**, or as you consume/buy more, WTP for additional unit declines
* A change in the **quantity demanded** is the result of **movement of the demand curve** at differing prices, ceteris paribus
* If NOT ceteris paribus, at a fixed price, this is a **demand curve shift** and is due to something other than price
	* E.g. increase/decrease in buyers.
	* Change in income (pos/neg shocks) shifts:
		* This is a **normal good** if a positive shock -> more buying since an individual's purchasing power has increased.
		* If income increases -> stop buying it is an **inferior good** because they switch to buying something else
	* RELATED GOODS SHIFTS:
		* If 2 goods are **complements**, demand shifts to the right (toast and jam) because you will buy both to meet one need (jam toast)
		* If 2 goods are **substitutes**, demand shifts to the left (jam vs jelly, one or the other)
	* Demand can be affected by buyer's beliefs in the future (bad storming coming? urgency affects demand because you can't go to the store for the duration of the storm)
	* Network and congestion effects are when the actions of others impact your demand for something (how does your use of social media depend on others?). Network means others increase demand, congestion decreases demand (there's so many people using this that I don't want to)

**Aggregation:** the process of adding up individual behaviors to calculate **market demand curves**. How you calculate market demand, add up everyone's WTP
	At $4 per jar, if one person would buy 1 jar and another would buy 2, the market demand is 3

**Law of demand:** Quantity demand rises when price falls
	Note: not always true but usually

## Seller Behavior
Two variables:
* Price
* The number of items they want to sell, or the quantity supplied

This can be modeled using a **supply schedule**
Side by side column chart

Also modeled by a **supply curves** as a typical horizontal/vertical axis graph. Usually upward sloping / upwards
* Price as y quantity as x
* Models **willingness to accept**: Lowest price a seller is willing to be paid for an extra unit of the good
* Can be affected by economy at scale but for simplicity not a factor for right now
* A change in PRICE ALONE will generate **movement along the supply curve**, but these factors shift the entire curve
* Shifts to the supply curve:
	* Change in the price of inputs used to produce the good (higher costs shifts left, lower right)
	* Changes in technology making the process more efficient
	* Changes in number of sellers
	* Sellers' beliefs about the future

Aggregation for a **market supply curve** works roughly the same. At a given price, how many of x will the market produce? Sum all individual supply curves

**Law of supply:** quantity supplied rises when price rises

# EQUILIBRIUM
The market in equilibrium we will be studying is simplified to **perfectly competitive**
* Sellers have identical goods
* No one can affect market price - everyone is a *price taker* (no negotiation on buyer or seller end)
* Summary:
	* Demand for a quantity of a product falls as the price rises
	* Supply of the product rises and the price rises
* Results in:
	* No buyers are willing to pay the price suppliers are willing to accept for the next unit
	* No sellers are willing to accept the price that the marginal buyer is willing to pay

**Arbitrage** is the process of competition driving down prices towards the equilibrium point (Q* and P* so perfect quantity and price)

*Example: Find equilibrium point*
Chocolate milk market
$Demand: Q_D=90-5P$
$Supply:Q_S=10P$

Graph will consist of a demand line and a supply line
Demand line will be \ starting at (0,18) going down to (90,0)
Supply line going up like / starting at (0,0) and going up to (18,90)
Calculate equilibrium point: $10P=90-50P\to6=P$ so point is (60,6)

*Example: Compensate for supply shift*
Costs a 1.50 more dollars to make chocolate milk (building off last one). Shifts supply line left

Mathematical shift:
$Q_S=10(P-1.5)$
$Q_D=90-5P$ note: doesn't change because it was a SUPPLY shift
Willingness to accept equation/equilibrium point: $P=\frac{Q_s}{10}+1.5$

$10P-15=90-5P$
$15P=105$
$P=7$
Plug into $Q_D$
Point is (55, 7)
