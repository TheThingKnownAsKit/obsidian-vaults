## Types of Economics

| Type | Positive | Normative |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **Micro** | Describing what individuals economic agents will probably do/have done | Ethical judgements of what individual economic agents should do |
| **Macro** | Describing what the economy will probably do/has done | Ethical judgements of what the economy should do |

| Type | Positive | Normative |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **Micro** | How does immigration affect wages of U.S. citizens? | Should we restrict immigration? |
| **Macro** | Does government spending lower unemployment during a recession? | Should Congress pass a "stimulus" spending bill? |

## Marginal Analysis
**Marginal analysis** is especially good for optimizing what the cost one ONE MORE unit would be, or specific amounts
You can apply marginal analysis using an iterative process. Break the decision into a series of smaller **marginal choices**.
At each stage: Would the **marginal benefits** of one more unit outweigh the **marginal costs**? If no, stop. If yes, continue to the next iteration

**Optimization in levels**: Tally the TOTAL net costs, pick the best option
**Optimization in changes**: Calculate the CHANGE in net benefits when moving from one alternative to another
They should give the same answer, one might just be easier in some situations
