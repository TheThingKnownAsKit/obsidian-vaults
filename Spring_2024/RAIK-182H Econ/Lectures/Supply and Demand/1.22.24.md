# Intro to Economics
NOTE: For this entire thing, costs/benefits are not necessarily always money. I just refuse to repeatedly restate that

An **economic agent** is an individual or group that makes choices.
Economic agents make choices about **scarce resources**. Things people want where the quantity desired exceeds the quantity available.

There are three main economic principles:
* Optimization
	* What is the best choice here for the economic agent? They will pick the closest one they can find to the best choice
* Equilibrium
	* A situation in which no agent would benefit personally by changing his or her own behavior. They cannot do any better by picking other options, everyone is optimized.
* Empiricism
	* Data driven

Optimization is frequently done with **cost-benefit analysis**
1. Evaluate the full set of costs and benefits of a choice
2. Make that choice only if the benefits are at least as large as the costs

**Incentives** shape decisions made by economic agents.

Your **willingness to pay (WTP)** is exactly what it sounds like.

**Economic surplus** is if you pay less for something you would have paid more for. It's cost was less than your WTP. 

**Framing effects** are things that distort their assessment of costs, benefits, etc. Common mistake people make when using cost-benefit analysis
	Grocery stores, just the whole thing all of it

**Opportunity cost** is the best alternative use of a resource. The costs that are not just monetary. 
Does not include **sunk costs**, or costs already spent. These are not included in cost-benefit analysis and opportunity cost.
