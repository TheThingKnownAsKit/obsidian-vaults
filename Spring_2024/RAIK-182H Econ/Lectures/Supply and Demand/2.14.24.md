The invisible hand 

A **pareto efficient** outcome is a situation in which no economic agent can be assisted without harming another
**Pareto improvement** is when you can help someone without hurting anyone else. Efficient happens when there is no more improvements left

People joining market shifts supply left to the old average long term something until it is neutral profit.

At an economy-wide scale, an alternative to the "invisible hand" is **central planning**. This would be when prices and buyer/seller combos are set by a controlling force, not by the economy
Challenges:
* **Coordination**: How could the planner know sellers' costs or the value buyers place on a good?
* **Incentives**: If a seller is guaranteed to get a profitable trade, what reason do you have to keep costs low?

In a free-market, an alternative approach is to institute **price controls**. 
Two kinds:
* **Price floor**: goods/services cannot be sold below this price
	* Minimum price for agricultural products. The government can't let too many farmers go out of business
* **Price ceiling**: goods/services cannot be sold above this price
	* Rent control policies
	* Can cause shortages and deadweight loss.
		* A **shortage** is when the intersection of the price ceiling and supply is before/less than where the demand intersects price ceiling.
		* More people are willing to pay than suppliers can supply (given optimal pricing for suppliers)

**Deadweight loss** is the social surplus lost due to unoptimal pricing/supplying. Typically due to price control

**Producer surplus** is the distance between the supply curve and the equilibrium price line. The total of producer surplus and consumer surplus (demand to price line) is **social surplus**

**Production possibilities frontier** is the line that represents all the possible combinations of goods/products within a budget
