## Production Function
IN THE SHORT RUN:
We will assume physical capital is a **fixed factor of production**. It will not increase/decrease unless specified
Workers can be hired/fired, so they are a **variable factor**

A **production function** is an equation that turns inputs into output goods. It will use the above simplifications to form a graph with output on the vertical axis and worker hours on the horizontal axis

**Marginal product**: How much more output will I get from 1 more unit of input (in this case number of workers)?
* The slope of the production function
* Example with workers in a restaurant: There is a slightly steeper curve at the beginning because with enough workers you can use **specialization** where each person has a job. Then, the **law of diminishing returns** will kick in and make the marginal product slower. Eventually, it'll become a negative marginal production because there's too many people in one space that it slows everything down

**Marginal revenue** is the net benefits from producing one more unit. The price of the product/service (because this is a perfectly competitive market)
The marginal revenue curve is flat (from the firm's perspective) (from market's perspective in a demand curve itd slope down)

$Profit=Revenue-Cost$
The optimal amount of products to produce for optimal profit is where $MarginalRevenue=MarginalCost$
This is because up until it because more expensive than profitable to make one more unit of product, it is worth it to make more units

## Supply Elasticity
Imagine the MR curve shifting up and down with the MC curve also graphed.
The **price elasticity of supply** is $\epsilon_{S}=\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}$

Low elasticity tends to correlate with a product that has mostly fixed costs and very little variable costs 
High elasticity tends to correlate with a product that has very few fixed costs and high variables costs

$Profit=(P-ATC)*Q$ is just the short term graph where things are profitable?
**Production surplus** is the area of the trapezoid that the MC of supply and intercept with the MR make

## Equations
Examples:
Production function:
$Q=F(K,L)=K^{\frac{2}{3}}L^{\frac{1}{3}}$
Where K is capital and L is labor

Total costs:
$TC=rK+wL$
Where r is rental cost, K is capital, and wL is worker costs

$L=\frac{Q^{3}}{K^{2}}\to TC=rK+w\frac{Q^{3}}{k=K^{2}}$
The derivative of this equation is marginal cost
$MC=\frac{dTC}{dQ}\to MC=3w\frac{Q^2}{K^3}$

Fixed costs is when Q = 0

$\pi = PQ-rK-w\frac{Q^3}{K^2}$
Pi is profit, PQ is revenues, rK is fixed costs, whole w term is variable costs
Profit function is a hill shape
The optimal quantity for price point on this graph is the top of the profit curve, where the derivative = 0 (local max). The derivative is: $\frac{d\pi}{dQ}=P-MC=0$ where P is marginal cost. Marginal revenue = marginal cost at the highest point
