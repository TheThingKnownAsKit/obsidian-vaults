Demand curve shifts:
* Changes in taste and preferences
* Income and wealth
* Changes in price
* Availability of related goods
* Number of buyers (remember demand is total number of sales)
* Network, congestion effects

Supply curve shifts:
* Change in price of inputs
* Changes in technology
* Number of sellers
* Sellers' beliefs about the future

Equilibrium:
* Put supply + demand and you get a **market**
* Markets are a study of a **perfectly competitive** market
	* Sellers have identical goods
	* No one can affect the market price directly (it'll fall naturally due to supply vs demand)
* P* and Q* represent the optimal price and quantity to get the most profit in equilibrium

The **buyer's problem**: how do consumers decide what to buy?
The economic approach considers three factors:
1. What do you like?
2. What are the prices?
3. What is your budget?

A **budget set** is a way to compare two options. Set a budget, put two items both in quantity on an x,y graph. Put the maximum amount of each in the given budget as starting points and draw a line. The triangle is the number of possible purchase combinations for quantity in the budget. Hypotenuse line is called the **budget constraint**

**Utility** is a measure of satisfaction or happiness. It is how economists measure preference. In units of **utils**
**Utility function**: an equation that turns stuff into utility. There is no set function
* Example: U = E, or utils = numEggs. So getting one egg = one more util
* Better example: $U=\sqrt{E}$ which captures the idea of **diminishing marginal benefits/utility** (the utils from your first egg is greater than your 50th, things get old)
