# BUYER'S PROBLEM
INCOME CHANGE:
Usually (not always) **income effects** -> buy more of a good when your income rises
	Exception for inferior goods

PRICE CHANGE:
Two opposing forces (if price of one good lowers):
* Lower price -> **substitution effect** in favor of more of good A and less of good B
* But also an **income effect** in favor of more everything (normal goods) because you're effectively richer
* You'll notice that price change has the exact same effect on the graph as income change (i think)
	* NOTE: they won't look exactly the same but they should have the same slope/shape

# ELASTICITY AND DEMAND
Buyer's problem directly influences demand

Remember: quantity demand falls because:
* Higher prices -> can't afford as much
* Higher prices -> you buy more substitutes instead
However, not everything has an easy substitute
	
**Elasticity** is the measure of the sensitivity of demand to price
	If you increase elasticity, the more likely the rubber band is to slip and hit you in the face for increasing price
	Multiple kinds
Right now, focus on the **price elasticity of demand**. It is a %change in quantity demanded because of a % change in price
$\epsilon_D=\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}$
When the demand elasticity is $\epsilon_D< 1$ then it is considered **inelastic** and consumers respond weakly to price change. Very steep graph
When the demand elasticity is $\epsilon_D> 1$ this is **elastic** and consumers respond strongly to price changes. Almost a flat line
**Unit elastic** is when it is exactly 1

## Calculating Elasticity
* One inconvenient fact about elasticity is that the calculation depends on your starting point
	* How elastic is demand in the specified region of this demand curve?
	* Using higher point as starting point usually yields high elasticity
	* Using a lower point as starting point usually yields lower elasticity
		* NOTE: Usually drop the minus sign

IN HOMEWORK/EXAMS WE WILL BE USING ARC ELASTICITIES TO GET AROUND THIS
* Use the average quantities and prices within a region to calculate the elasticity (the middle of the graph)
	* Invariant to the starting point

## What affects price elasticity?
Things that affect price elasticity:
* More available **substitutes** -> more elastic demand
* Higher **share of your budget** -> more elastic demand
* More **time available to adjust** to the new prices -> more elastic demand

## Cross-price elasticity of demand
How does demand respond to changes in prices of other goods and services?
This is **cross-price elasticity of demand**
$\epsilon_{xy}=\frac{\%\Delta Q_x}{\%\Delta P_y}$

Negative $\epsilon_{xy}$ means they are not substitutes they are complements
Positive means they are substitutes 

## Income elasticity of demand
Instead of the price changing, when income changes we get **income elasticity of demand**
$\epsilon_I=\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta Income}$

Negative $\epsilon_I$ means an inferior good
Positive $\epsilon_I$ means a normal good
