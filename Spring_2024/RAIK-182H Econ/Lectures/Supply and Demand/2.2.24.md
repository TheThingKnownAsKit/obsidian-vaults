Indifference curves:
Buyer tradeoffs can be modeled using **indifference curves**. The set of all "bundles" that provide an equal level of satisfaction
	3 utils + 1 util is the same as 2 utils + 2 utils
	Equivalent exchanges
Generally downward-slopping, but shapes depend on preference
Technically 3d but just keep in mind that northeast = more utils (x and y are taken up by goods a and b)

Indifference curves and **perfect complements** (products you need to buy as pairs) looks more like half a square
	L

If you look at a graph of substitutes, it's perfectly linear forming a triangle because all these products provide the exact same utils per unit

If you dislike one of the goods, it'll turn concave because you have to give them more of good A to compensate for having to use good B. Most of the time it's assumed the buyer likes both goods, though

The optimal choice on an indifference curve is **tangent** to the budget constraint. The budget curve is the tangent to the choice

The **marginal rate of substitution** is the slope of the indifference curve
	The amount of good X you are willing to give up for a unit of Y
	Because of diminishing marginal utility, the ratio of the marginal utilities of each good
$MRS_{XY}=\frac{MU_X}{MU_Y}$
The MRS is the derivative of the utility function. You take the derivative with respect to one specific product and then assume the other is a constant so it drops out
The MRS = budget curve slope

$\frac{MB_E}{P_E}=\frac{MN_o}{P_o}$
$\frac{MU_e}{P_e}=\frac{MU_o}{P_o}$
