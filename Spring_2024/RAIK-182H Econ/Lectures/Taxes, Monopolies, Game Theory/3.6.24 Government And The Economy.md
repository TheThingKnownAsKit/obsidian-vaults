The **average tax rate** is the total tax paid as a share of before-tax income
	How much of your total income are you spending on taxes?
	$\frac{TotalTaxes}{IncomeBeforeTaxes}$
The **marginal tax rate** is the tax paid on the last dollar earned
	If you work one more hour, how much taxes are you paying on that money?
	This is what the US tax system is based on

A **progressive tax system** is the idea that if you have a higher income you should pay more marginal taxes. Get graph from slides. Each step up in taxation rates once an income threshold is met is called a **kink**
	Most income taxes for countries including US
A **regressive tax system** is when people with lower income pay more taxes
	Excise taxes by accident (not a policy a byproduct of the economy?)
**Proportional** is when there's one tax rate everyone pays
	Social security taxes

How do taxes affect the market?

Remember the supply curve represents the marginal cost of producing one more unit of the good
*A tax is essentially just another production cost that would shift the supply curve left/up by the cost of the tax*
This creates deadweight loss and tax revenue on a standard supply and demand graph
	Tax revenue is the box 
	Deadweight loss is the triangle next to the boss

**Tax incidence** refers to the question of "who pays for a tax". Answer: everyone basically
**Remit** is directly paying the tax
	Paying the tax
**Incidence** is indirectly paying the tax
	Taxes increasing production costs increase the cost of goods for buyers

If you make the consumers remit the taxes then it would shift the demand curve left/down

*In a perfectly competitive market, making a producer or consumer remit taxes makes no difference to the final outcome*
