# INTERMEDIATE MARKET STRUCTURES
**Monopolistic competition** is many firms with differentiated products
* Competitive in the sense that entry and exit is relatively easy
* Monopolistic in the sense that they face a downward-sloping demand curve for their products
Firms in this kind of market have high price-setting power because there is no perfect substitute for their product (arby's). This results in prices getting set very similarly to a monopoly

An **oligopoly** is a market structure where there is few firms but not one like in a monopoly
* With homogenous products: oil, cement
* With differentiated products: cigarettes, cars

The key to understanding intermediate market structures is **residual demand**. Once you find the **residual demand curve** then optimizing price and quantity is possible

# FIXING MONOPOLIES DEADWEIGHT LOSS
Monopolies create inefficiency

The private market can recover social surplus through **price discrimination**. This means charging different prices to different consumers, as opposed to the **law of one price** we have been using so far. If the discriminated price is based on willingness to pay, it is **perfect price discrimination**.
![[Pasted image 20240320131606.png]]
This creates higher social surplus because there is no deadweight loss, but completely eliminates consumer surplus

Types of price discrimination not perfect:
* **Second degree** is when price is based on the characteristics of the purchase
	* Was it bought for a time frame that's indicative?
		* Plane tickets bought on weekdays can be assumed to be business purchases and are therefore less elastic, while tickets bought on the weekend are probably for family vacations and are then more elastic
	* Was it bought in bulk?
* **Third degree** is when price is based on the attributes of the buyer, such as age or gender
	* Student discounts, elderly discounts, insurance for teenagers

---

It can also be fixed through policy

Policies aimed at preventing anticompetitive pricing are known as **antitrust** laws
They also break up monopolies to restore competition
	They can also restrict mergers and reduce legal protections like patents for very large companies if they don't outright break them up

The government can also institute price controls to try and impose a **socially optimal** or **fair returns** price
