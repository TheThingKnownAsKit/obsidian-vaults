## General Systems of Linear Equations
A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linear equation in n variables</mark> $x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}$is an equation that can be written in the form $a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}=b$ where a and b are constants

An n-tuple (x1,x2,...,xn) is a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">solution</mark> to a linear equation if the equality is indeed true

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">system of n linear equations with n variables</mark> is written the same as above but with more

A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">solution</mark> of a linear system in variables is an n-tuple that satisfies every equation in the system
All solutions to a system of linear equations is called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">solution set</mark>

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Two systems of linear equations are said to be equivalent if they have the same solution set</mark>
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Given a system of linear equations, any of the three elementary row operations performed on the system produces an equivalent system</mark>

### Example - Unique solution
$$\begin{matrix}
3x-y+z=0 \\
2x+y+2z=2 \\
x+4y-2z=11
\end{matrix}$$
Use elementary row operations to produce the result. We want the top equation to have only x, the middle to only have y, and the bottom to only have z

1. Swap rows 1 and 3 because row 3 is set up really easy to solve for x
$$\begin{matrix}
x+4y-2z=11 \\
2x+y+2z=2 \\
3x-y+z=0
\end{matrix}$$

2. You can eliminate x from rows 2 and 3 by multiplying row 1 by -2 and -3, respectively. $R_{2}-2R_{1}\text{ and }R_{3}-3R_{1}$. Below is after subtracting
$$\begin{matrix}
x+4y-2z=11 \\
-7y+6z=-20 \\
-13y+7z=-33
\end{matrix}$$

3. We want to get the 2nd equation so that there is a 1 in the y coefficient. We don't want to work with fractions but you could just multiply by -1/7. Instead we're going to multiply the 2nd equation by -2 to get even numbers. $-2R_{2}$
$$\begin{matrix}
14y-12z=40 \\
R_{2}+R_{3} \\
y-5z=7 \\
 \\
x+4y-2z=11 \\
y-5z=7 \\
-13y+7z=-33
\end{matrix}$$

4. Isolate the z with $R_{3}+13R_{2}$
$$\begin{matrix}
-58z=58 \\
x=-1
\end{matrix}$$

5. This gives us:
$$\begin{matrix}
x+4y-2z=11 \\
y-5z=7 \\
z=-1
\end{matrix}$$
Which is VERY close to the solution. Substitute back in backwards from z
$$\begin{matrix}
x+4(2)-2(-1)=11\to x=1 \\
y-5(-1)=7\to y=2 \\
z=-1
\end{matrix}$$

The solution is $\begin{matrix}x=1 \\ y=2 \\ z=-1\end{matrix}$

### Example - Infinitely Many Solutions

Notice we have 3 unknowns and only 2 equations. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">This means one of two things: these planes could be parallel or they could intersect in a line</mark>, so either none or infinite solutions

We already have an x in R1 so we want to get rid of the one in R2, hence $R_{2}-R_{1}$

This gives us as few as possible variables in each equation, you cannot eliminate any more. The only thing you can do is solve for x & y for z

For infinite solutions:
1. Solve for as many variables as you can in terms of one of the variables
2. Set that one variable equal to a parameter (t usually) to get the infinite solutions
