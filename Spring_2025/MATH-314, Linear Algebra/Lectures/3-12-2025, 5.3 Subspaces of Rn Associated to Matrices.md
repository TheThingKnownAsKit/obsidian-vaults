## Row Space
Let A be an m x n matrix. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">row space</mark> of A, denoted by row(A), is the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ spanned by the rows of A.

Example, $A=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 \\ -1 & 0 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
$row(A)=span(\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}-1&0&4\end{bmatrix})$
$RREF(A)=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & -4 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{7}{2}\end{bmatrix}$
So, the span is 2?

Note:
If matrix B was obtained from A by an elementary row operation, then the following are true:
$$row(A)=row(B)$$
$$row(A)=row(RREF(A))$$

Example:
The basis for the row space of the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & -3\end{bmatrix}$
$RREF(A)=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
$basis:\{\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\}$

## Column Space
Let A be an m x n matrix. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">column space</mark> of A, denoted by col(A, is the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$ spanned by the columns of A.

Given a matrix B, we can <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">compute a basis for col(B) using the same method for computing the basis of a spanning set</mark>
1. Compute an REF of B
2. Pivot columns of B form a basis for col(B)

## Null Space
Let A be an m x n matrix. The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">null space</mark> of A, denoted by null(A), is the set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ of all vectors $\overrightarrow{x}$ that satisfy $A\overrightarrow{x}=\overrightarrow{0}$

Example: compute the null space of the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2 & 2 \\ -3 & -3\end{bmatrix}$
RREF: $$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}\to x+y=0\implies \begin{matrix}
x=-t \\
y=t
\end{matrix}$$
$$\overrightarrow{x}=\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}t$$
$$null(A)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}t:t \epsilon \mathbb{R}\right\}=span(\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
1
\end{bmatrix})$$

Let A be an m x n matrix. Then null(A) is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ It is closed under addition and closed under scalar multiplication

## Rank-Nullity Theorem
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">nullity</mark> of a matrix A is $nullity(A)=dim(null(A))$
Suppose A is an m x n matrix. Then $rank(A)+nullity(A)=n$
