## Coordinate Vectors
Recall i and j as the standard unit vectors of [1, 0] and [0, 1]

We can describe other vectors as linear combinations of these vectors.
$$\overrightarrow{v}=\begin{bmatrix}
a \\
b
\end{bmatrix}=a\begin{bmatrix}
1 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+b\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}$$

When doing this, we say a and b are <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">coordinates</mark> of v with respect to {i, j} and $\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix}$ is the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">coordinate vector</mark> of v with respect to {i, j}

### Example
Find the coordinates of the vector $\overrightarrow{v}=\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}$ with respect to $\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}\right\}$.

1. Put the coordinate vectors in with scalars and set it equal to v
$c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}+c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}$

2. Put it in matrix form and solve for REF
$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & | & 2 \\
0 & 2 & | & 6
\end{bmatrix}\to \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & | & 2 \\
0 & 1 & | & 3
\end{bmatrix}$$

3. Put into linear equation form and solve
$$\begin{matrix}
c_{2}=3 \\
c_{1}+c_{2}=2\to c_{1}+3=2\to c_{1}=-1
\end{matrix}$$

Therefore, the coordinate vector of v with respect to the given vectors is $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}$

## Basis of R^N or a Subspace
A set R of vectors is called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">basis</mark> of $\mathbb{R}^n$ if:
1. $Span(R)=\mathbb{R}^n$ <-- existence question
2. R is linearly independent <-- uniqueness

Similarly, a basis of a subspace V of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a set of vectors S that
1. $Span(S)=V$
2. S is linearly independent

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Remember its span is how many leading entries it has and to show linear independence we need a leading entry in every column</mark>
Basically this means to be a basis of something it has to have a leading entry in every COLUMN and ROW of REF
Leading entry in every COLUMN = linearly independent
Leading entry in every ROW = spans that R

### Example (True)
Determine if the set $\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}\right\}$ is the basis of $\mathbb{R^2}$

1. Put into a matrix and solve for REF
$$\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}$$
Oh wow it's already in REF how convenient

There's a leading entry in every row so it spans R^2 and a leading entry in every column so the set is linearly independent. It IS a basis

### Example (False)
Determine if the sets $A=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}\right\}$ and $B=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}\right\}$ form a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$

A. notice it's already in REF
It is linearly independent because there is a leading entry in every column, but it does NOT span R^2 because there is only 1 leading entry

B. notice it's already in REF as $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$
Spans R^2 because there is a leading entry in every row (of which there's 2) but it is NOT linearly independent because there is not a leading entry for every column (column 3 is a free variable)

These do NOT form the basis

### Example (Find a Basis)
Find a basis for the subspace $V=span\left(\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}\right)$

1. Put it in a matrix and solve for REF
$$\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -2
\end{bmatrix}\to \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & -2 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}$$
It is not linearly independent because not every column has a leading entry. Notice that the first two vectors ARE linearly independent and span R^2

So, $V=Span\left(\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}\right)$ and these two vectors are a basis for V

Helpful observations:
- If a linear independent set of vectors in R^n contains m vectors, then $m\leq n$
- Let V be a linearly independent set of vectors. If u is not in the span of V, then the set V is linearly independent
- Let V be a subspace. Then any linearly independent subset of V can be extended to a basis for V
