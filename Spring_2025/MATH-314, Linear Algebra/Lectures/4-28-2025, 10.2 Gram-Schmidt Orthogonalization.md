Let $\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}\}$ be a basis for a subspace W in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The set of vectors below form an orthogonal basis for the same subspace
$\left\{f_{1}=v_{1},f_{2}=f_{2}-\frac{v_{2}*f_{1}}{f_{1}*f_{1}}f_{1},\dots,f_{k}=v_{k}-\frac{v_{k}*f_{1}}{f_{1}*f_{1}}f_{1}-\dots-\frac{v_{k}*f_{k-1}}{f_{k-1}*f_{k-1}}f_{k-1}\right\}$
It's easier to visual it vertically
$\begin{matrix}f_{1}=v_{1} \\ f_{2}=v_{2}-\frac{f_{1}*v_{2}}{f_{1}*f_{1}}f_{1} \\ f_{3}=v_{3}-\frac{f_{2}*v_{3}}{f_{2}*f_{2}}f_{2}-\frac{f_{1}*v_{3}}{f_{1}*f_{1}}f_{1} \\ f_{n}=v_{n}-\frac{f_{n-1}*v_{n}}{f_{n-1}*f_{n-1}}f_{n-1}-\dots-\frac{f_{1}v_{n}}{f_{1}*f_{1}}f_{1}\end{matrix}$

Basically it's a process for taking a basis, applying the gram-schmidt process to it, and then getting an orthogonal basis
