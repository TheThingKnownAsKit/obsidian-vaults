## Closure
A set V is said to be <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">closed under addition</mark> if for all elements $u,v\epsilon V$ (vectors u and v in V), then
$$\overrightarrow{u}+\overrightarrow{v}\epsilon V$$

A set V is said to be <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">closed under scalar multiplication</mark> if for each $v\epsilon V$ and scalar $k\epsilon\mathbb{R}$, then:
$$k\overrightarrow{v}\epsilon V$$

### Example 1: Closed Under Multiplication (False)
$E=\{\dots,-4,-2,00,2,4,\dots\}$
$\{2n:n\epsilon\mathbb{Z}\}$

We need to show that $k*2n\epsilon E$ for general for general k in R and n in Z or we have to give a counterexample showing E is not closed under scalar multiplication

If k = 0.3 and n = 3, $0.5*2*3=3$ which is NOT in E. So, E is NOT closed under scalar multiplication

### Example 2: Closed Both (False)
Let $X_{1}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}\epsilon\mathbb{R^2}:x_{1},x_{2}\geq_{0}\right\}$. Determine if X is closed under addition and scalar multiplication

Let $\overrightarrow{u},\overrightarrow{v}\epsilon X_{1}\implies \begin{matrix}\overrightarrow{u}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix},x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0 \\ \overrightarrow{v}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{1} \\ y_{2}\end{bmatrix},y_{1}y_{2}\geq 0\end{matrix}$

$\overrightarrow{u}+\overrightarrow{v}\geq 0$
Closed under addition. Both vectors are defined to be >= 0, so adding them will also always be >= 0

Now for multiplication:
$k=-1\epsilon\mathbb{R},\overrightarrow{u}=\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ z\end{bmatrix}\epsilon X_{1}$
$k\overrightarrow{u}=-1\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$ which is NOT in X as defined above
Not closed under addition

X1 is closed under addition but not closed under multiplication

## Vector Space

Further, $\mathbb{R}^n$ is closed under both addition and multiplication

These 8 properties with closure mean that $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">vector space</mark>

## Subspaces of R^n
Let V be a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is closed under both vector addition and scalar multiplication. Then V is a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">subspace</mark> of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

![[Pasted image 20250310095555.png]]

To prove something is a subspace, prove it is closed under both conditions
