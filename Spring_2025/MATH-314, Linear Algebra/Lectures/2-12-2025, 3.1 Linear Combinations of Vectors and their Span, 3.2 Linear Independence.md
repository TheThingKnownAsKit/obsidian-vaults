## Linear Combinations
A vector v is a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linear combination</mark> of vectors $v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}$ if $\overrightarrow{v}=a_{1}\overrightarrow{v_{1}}+a_{2}\overrightarrow{v_{2}}+\dots+a_{n}\overrightarrow{v_{n}}$ for some scalars a1, a2, ..., an

Basically a vector can be written as a linear combination if given weights with scalars

Example:
$$\begin{bmatrix}
-4 \\
9 \\
-10 \\
-1
\end{bmatrix}=2\begin{bmatrix}
-1 \\
3 \\
-3 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+(-1)\begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
0 \\
1 \\
4
\end{bmatrix}+3\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
1 \\
-1 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Example 1
Find $k_{1},k_{2}$ such that $k_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}+k_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$

Firstly, notice that
$$k_{1}\begin{bmatrix}
1 \\
2
\end{bmatrix}+k_{2}\begin{bmatrix}
1 \\
-1
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
k_{1} \\
2k_{2}
\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}
k_{2} \\
-k_{2}
\end{bmatrix}$$
Basically treat the k's as scalar values and just multiply them in

Combine the cells and substitute the numbers back in
$$\begin{bmatrix}
k_{1}+k_{2} \\
2k_{1}-k_{2}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
4 \\
-1
\end{bmatrix}therefore: \begin{matrix}
k_{1}+k_{2}=4 \\
2k_{1}-k_{2}=-1
\end{matrix}$$

We now know the individual vectors in terms of k and what they solve to, just not the actual scalar values. We can put this in a matrix and use RREF to solve for k

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&|&4 \\
2&-1&|&-1
\end{bmatrix}\underrightarrow{RREF}\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&|&1 \\
0&1&|&3
\end{bmatrix}$$
So therefore, k1 = 1 and k2 = 3

Or yknow just ignore a and b and solve the matrix's as the first step

### Example 2
Determine if $\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$ is a linear combination of $\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$

In other words, check if there are other solutions to $k_{1}\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}+k_{2}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$

Translate it into matrix form and solve through REF
$$\begin{bmatrix}
4 & -2 & | & 4 \\
2 & -1 & | & -1
\end{bmatrix}\underrightarrow{REF}\begin{bmatrix}
4 & -2 & | & 4 \\
0 & 0 & | & -3
\end{bmatrix}$$
No solution, so it is NOT a linear combination of the other two

## Span
Let $v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The set S of all linear combinations of these vectors is called the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">span</mark> of $v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}$

![[Pasted image 20250212175703.png]]

For nonzero vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the general spans are:
1. span (v) is a line
2. span (v, w) is a
	1. line if v and w are multiples of each other
	2. a plane otherwise
3. span (v1, v2, ..., vk) is a
	1. line if all vectors are multiples of each other
	2. plane if k-2 of the vectors are a linear combination of 2 vectors that are not multiples of each other
	3. or some other linear space

## Redundant Vectors

## Linear Independence
Let $v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The set $\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}$ is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linearly independent</mark> if the only solution to the linear combination equation
$$c_{1}\overrightarrow{v_{1}}+c_{2}\overrightarrow{v_{2}}+\dots+c_{k}\overrightarrow{v_{k}}=\overrightarrow{0}$$
is the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">trivial solution</mark> $c_{1}=c_{2}=\dots=c_{k}=0$

If there is a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">nontrivial solution</mark> (not all c's are zero) to the linear combination equation, then we say that the set is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linearly dependent</mark>
