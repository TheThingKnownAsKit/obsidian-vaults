## Linear Transformations from R^n to R^m
A transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linear transformation</mark> if
1. $T(\overrightarrow{u}+\overrightarrow{v})=T(\overrightarrow{u})+T(\overrightarrow{v})$
2. $T(k\overrightarrow{u})=kT(\overrightarrow{u})$
for all vectors u and v in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and scalars k

Theorem 6.2.10: Let $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}^m$ be a linear transformation. Then
1. $T(\overrightarrow{0})=\overrightarrow{0}$
2. T maps any line in $\mathbb{R}^n$ to a line (or the zero vector) in $\mathbb{R}^m$

Theorem 6.2.11: Let A be an m x n matrix. Define $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}^m$ by $T(v)=Av$. Then T is a linear transformation

## Linear Transformations of Subspaces
Let V and W be subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbb{R}^m$. A transformation $T:V\to W$ is called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">linear transformation</mark> if
1. $T(\overrightarrow{u}+\overrightarrow{v})=T(\overrightarrow{u})+T(\overrightarrow{v})$
2. $T(k\overrightarrow{u})=kT(\overrightarrow{u})$
For all vectors u and v in V and scalars k

![[Pasted image 20250327115423.png]]
![[Pasted image 20250327115431.png]]

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">identity transformation</mark> on V, denoted by $\text{id}_{v}$, is the transformation
$$id_{v}(\overrightarrow{v})=\overrightarrow{v}$$
Aka $id_{\mathbb{R}^n}(\overrightarrow{x})=I_{n}\overrightarrow{x}$

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">zero transformation</mark>, Z, is the transformation
$$Z(\overrightarrow{v})=\overrightarrow{0}$$
Aka $Z(\overrightarrow{x})=0\overrightarrow{x}$

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Theorem: The identity and zero transformations are linear</mark>

## Kernal of Linear Transformations
Let $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}^m$ be a linear transformation with standard matrix A. Then the <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">kernal</mark> of T, denoted $ker(T)$, is the set of all vectors $\overrightarrow{x}\in \mathbb{R}^n$ such that
$$T(\overrightarrow{x})=\overrightarrow{0}$$

Note that $ker(T)=null(A)$

The basis of the kernel is just the direction vector
