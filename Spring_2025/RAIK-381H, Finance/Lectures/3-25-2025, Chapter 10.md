A firm's <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)</mark> is a single number representing the cost of capital to the firm
$$WACC=R_{D}*(1-T)*\left( \frac{D}{E+D} \right)+R_{E}*\left( \frac{E}{E+D} \right)$$
$$WACC=R_{D}*(1-T)*W_{D}+R_{E}*W_{E}$$
- $R_{E}$ is the cost of equity
- $W_{E}$ is the fraction of the firm financed by equity (aka $\frac{E}{E+D}$)
- $R_{D}$ is the cost of debt
- $W_{D}$ is the fraction of the firm financed by debt (aka $\frac{D}{E+D}$)
- T is the tax rate of the firm
- E is the market value of equity
- D is the market value of debt
