A bond's <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">duration</mark> tells us how susceptible a bond is to interest rate risk
- The higher the duration, the MORE susceptible to interest rate risk the bond is
- The lower the duration, the LESS susceptible to interest rate risk the bond is

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Macaulay</mark> duration is a weighted average of the timing of the cash flows to represent bond duration
- Units in years
- A bond with a Macaulay duration of 4.25 is riskier than a bond with 3.75
- Higher maturity date -> higher durationn -> higher risk
- Lower coupon rate -> higher duration -> higher risk

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">modified duration</mark> is slightly modified Macaulay duration. It can help estimate things
![[Pasted image 20250227161108.png]]

$$\text{Modified Duration}=\frac{\text{Macaulay Duration}}{1+\frac{YTM}{N}}$$
Where YTM = yield to maturity and n = number of coupon periods per year

Duration is good for estimating change in bond value. For large change in yields, the estimation won't be perfect. For small changes, it's close
