Roll several dice at once and take the single highest result. If it's a 6, it's a full success. Two or more 6's is a CRITICAL success. 4-5 is a partial success. 1-3 is a bad outcome.
	You cannot crit if you have zero or negative dice to roll. Roll two dice and take the single lowest

1. State goal
2. Choose action rating (stat modifier)
3. GM sets position
4. GM sets effect level
5. Add bonus dice if applicable
6. Roll

**Action roll** = regular ability roll
**Downtime roll** = a roll to see how much someone gets done during a downtime activity
**Fortune roll** = luck (it's funky there's a subsection)
**Resistance roll** = a roll to reduce the severity of a consequence (it's also funky there is also a subsection)

# Positioning
When rolling, you can be in three positions: controlled, risky, and desperate

These positions affect the **severity level of consequences you may experience**. Controlled is minimal consequences, risky (which is the default) is moderate consequences, and desperate is severe consequences

- Controlled: You have a golden opportunity. You're exploiting a dominant advantage. You're set up for scucess
- Risky: You go head to head. You're acting under duress. You're taking a chance.
	- Default. It is odd to have controlled rolls since if it's too controlled it wouldn't be a roll at all, it'd just be a guaranteed success. It is odd for a character to deliberately choose a desperate roll either
- Desperate: You're in serious trouble. You're overreaching your capabilities. You're attempting a dangerous maneuver

# Resistance Rolls
You can roll to reduce the severity of a consequence. Resistance rolls ALWAYS succeed, it's just that the better you roll the less stress it costs to reduce or avoid the danger. You roll with your attribute rating in the relevant attribute (insight, prowess, and resolve) and you subtract your result from 6 and take that much stress

Resistance rolls can happens before or after your own action roll depending on enemy advantage

If you get a crit on a resistance roll, clear 1 stress

You can only roll against a consequence once

You can completely avoid a consequence depending

If multiple consequences are happening at once, you have to pick which one(s) you want to roll against. Can be all, none, or some

Armor is a free resistance roll to harm

# Effect Level
The effect level is how much this action can accomplish. What is the ceiling of achieve-ability here. Either limited, standard, or great

**Great**: You achieve more than usual. It comes with additional benefits. Ticks 3 segments
**Standard**: You achieve what is expected. Normal. Ticks 2
**Limited**: You achieve a partial or weak effect. Ticks 1
**Zero Effect**: After considering factors, effect level is reduced below limited
**Extreme Effect**: After considering factors, effect level is increased above great

To determine effect, assess potency, quality/tier, and scale.

**Potency**: Considers relevant weaknesses/strengths of the situation. For example, if you are using lightning against a ghost, that is particularly potent
**Quality/Tier**: Tools, weapons, and resources will have a quality/tier if that is relevant. Fine items count as +1 bonus, stacking with Tier. Typically, if you have a lower quality/tier and it's the main factor, it is limited. If you have same quality/tier, it's standard. If you exceed, it's great
**Scale**: Number of opponents, size of area, scope of influence, etc. Depends on the situation

If one effect factor overshadows the other, the side with that advantage dominates the situation and the rest of the factors become worthless. For example, it doesn't matter if you have a fine sword and extra effect if you try to fight 20 people at once (the scale dominates). The result should be limited or zero effect

Any PC abilities or choices that give +1 effect come into play AFTER GM assessment

**PCs can trade position for effect**. If they could only achieve a standard effect in a risky position, they might want to "push their luck" and roll for great effect in a desperate position

# Consequences
There are five types of consequences, reduced effect, complication, lost opportunity, worse position, and harm

**Reduced Effect**: The effect level is lowered by one after all other factors are accounted for
**Complication**: Trouble, mounting danger, or a new threat emerge. There might be a new problem (room catches fire, you're disarmed, crew takes +1 HEAT, lose status with a faction, etc). The GM can also tick a clock for the complication. A serious complication is more severe. Complications should never negate a successful role
**Lost Opportunity**: Current approach/opportunity is no longer viable, come up with a new plan
**Worse Position**: Your position lowers by one. Show escalating action. It can't decrease on paper, describe how the situation gets worse
**Harm**: Inflict lesser harm, moderate harm, or severe harm

# Fortune Roll's
Luck rolls :). They're weird

- Situations the PCs aren't directly involved with
- Uncertain outcomes

Fortune roll dice pools can use any trait rating, or tier, or quality or magnitude, or whatever. It's really vibes

Generally, it'll be 1d for each TRAIT raiting, +1d for each MAJOR ADVANTAGE, -1d for each MAJOR DISADVANTAGE

# Roll Add-On's
Bonus dice:
- Pushing yourself OR Devil's Bargain
	- Push Yourself: Take 2 stress per action chosen below (you can choose each once)
		- Add +1d to your roll
		- Add +1 level to your effect
		- Be able to perform an action while impaired by harm
	- Devil's Bargain: the GM or any other player can offer a bonus die if you accept some bad consequence
		- Add +1d to your roll
		- The consequence will happen regardless of the rolls outcome. Consequences can include collateral damage, unintended harm, spend coins or items, betray someone, offend a faction, start/tick a clock, add HEAT, suffer harm, etc
- Assistance
	- Teammate takes 1 stress, describes how they would help, and you get +1d
- Hunting grounds
	- You gain +1d to gather information when preparing to execute an operation of your preferred type on your hunting grounds (you also get an additional downtime activity)

You can get the above bonuses any time, and if you get additional bonuses from an ability or something it is in addition to these. They do not cancel out, they add together

# Flashbacks
They're weird! The rule's specifically DO NOT distinguish between actions made in the present or past. At any point in the score, invoke a flashback to roll for an action in the past that impacts your current situation

Costs stress.
- 0 STRESS: An ordinary action for which you had easy opportunity
- 1 STRESS: A complex action or unlikely opportunity
- 2 (or more) STRESS: An elaborate action that involved special opportunities or contingencies

Can be an action roll or fortune roll like normal, or no roll and you just pay the stress

If the flashback involves a downtime activity, pay 1 COIN or REP for it instead of stress

Notably cannot UNDO anything. Can ADD to something already happening

# Teamwork
Four teamwork maneuvers
1. *Assist* another PC who's doing an action roll
	1. 1 stress +1d
	2. Potentially shared consequences
	3. Can assist a group action only if you are not participating in it. One character in the group action gets the bonus die
2. Lead a *group action*
	1. Everyone makes an action roll with the same action. Single best result is the overall effort for everyone who rolled
	2. Character leading takes 1 stress for each PC that rolls 1-3 as their best
	3. Leading cohorts is a group action, cohort rolls its quality level. Roll COMMAND if you direct or appropriate rating if participate
3. *Protect* a teammate
	1. Suffer a consequence instead of a teammate
	2. You can roll to resist like normal
4. *Set up* a character who will follow through on your action
	1. Roll an appropriate action roll. If success, +1 effect level OR improved position (PC choice)
	2. Does not have to be the same action roll for the PC you are setting up for
