## Data Collection
An <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">active collection</mark> is like an experiment or survey etc
A <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">passive collection</mark> is observation
Generally, there is no single list or fully-agreed upon set of all collection techniques. You can make up a new one to suit your needs
	By doing CITI training, you are eligible to do research with human data (included as one of the parts of your project later in the course)

When working with data, especially from human subjects, you often need to <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">clean the data</mark>
* Removing typos, standardizing units and formulas, handling missing data uniformly, etc
You can improve data quality by improving the questions you ask and planning ahead
* <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Likert scales</mark> are useful for asking opinion questions. Each option should have a label and the range should go from one opposite to another. Usually Strongly Disagree, Slightly Disagree, Neutral, etc

f you are working with free-form responses like text. You can use <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">thematic analysis</mark> where you:
1. Code the data (annotate)
2. Group codes into concepts
3. Group concepts into categories
4. Determine theory based off categories
