Non-mutually exclusive questions

Generalized Axioms
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">General Addition Rule</mark> for any events A and B is: $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">General Subtraction Rule</mark> is $P(\overline{A})=1-P(A))$
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Multiplication rule for independent events</mark> is $P(A\cap B)=P(A)*P(B)$
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Multiplication rule for ANY events</mark> A and B is $P(A \cap B)=P(A)*P(B|A)$
	P(B|A) read as the probability of B given A.It is the probability of an event happening under the assumption that another dependent event has happened
	This is called a <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">conditional</mark> probability
	NOTE: Probability depends on known information. If the information on the outcomes is unknown, it will default to pretending they are independent events

Remember:
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Union</mark> is the joining of sets (no duplicates) -> $\cup$ `\cup`
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Intersection</mark> only includes the outcomes that are in both sets -> $\cap$ `\cap`

In cases of random selection with equal chances, it is # desired outcomes/possible ones

For non-zero probability events, the following statements are true:
1.<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">If they are mutually exclusive, they are not independent -> they are dependent</mark>
	1. Obtaining an outcome from one will affect the probability of the other-it will be 0 because they cannot co-occur
	2. Example, flipping a single coin
2. <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">If they are independent events, they are not mutually exclusive -> they are independent</mark>

Note mutually exclusive is NOT the same as independent. It is not functionally useful to make the distinction but it can technical happen
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">It IS possible for an event to be NEITHER mutually exclusive nor independent</mark>
