## Conditional Probability
P(A|B) is read as the probability of A given B

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Conditional probability</mark> is $P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$
For independent events

![[Pasted image 20240925154929.png]]

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Bayes' Rule</mark> there may be times when we know P(B|A) but we want to solve for P(A|B). Allows us to relate the two in a way where we can solve
$P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$

## Permutations and Combinations
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Intersection is commutative</mark>, aka $A\$

Conditional probability(in set notation) is $P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{P(B\cap A)}{P(B)}$
By the multiplication rule, $P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{P(B)*P(A|B)}{P(B)}=P(A|B)$

The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Law of Total Probability</mark> is if the sample space is partitioned into two or more MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE subevents: probability can be expressed as conditional probabilities given each of the subevents
