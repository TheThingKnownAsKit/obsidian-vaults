## PDFs
The probabilities associated with values of continuous random variables are described by <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">probability density functions (pdf)</mark>
Unlike pmf, pdfs do not represent the probability at a particular point. It is the DENSITY of probability, so the probability is given by the area under the curve for a particular range
Pdfs must always be greater than 0. No negative probabilities under the curve
	Use integrals

Inclusive or exclusive bounds do not matter

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">To find the value that makes 0 < x < c a valid pdf, take the integral and set it = 1 and solve</mark>

For continuous random variables, to find an expected value just do x * p(x), but don't forget to integrate f(x) to get the probability

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">For a pdf to be valid, the total sum of probabilities (integral) of a < x < b must = 1</mark>
