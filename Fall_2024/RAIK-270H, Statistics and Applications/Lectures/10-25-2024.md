## Expected Values
For discrete random variables, we calculated a weighted average based on the probabilities of each value:
$$\mu=\sum\limits(x*p(x))$$
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">For continuous, you do the same thing but you have to integrate f(x) first</mark>
$$\mu=\int^{max}_{min}x*f(x)dx$$

## Variance and Standard Deviation
To get the variance, you take the integral of the average from the mean squared. It can be simplified if you take mu out
$$\sigma=(\int^{max}_{min}x^2*f(x)dx)-\mu^2$$

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Mu is the average value, kinda a constant so it can be pulled out.</mark> Same thing as expected value calculated above

Standard deviation is just sqrt of variance

<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">For exponential distribution, the mean is:</mark>
$$E(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x*\lambda e^{\lambda x}dx=\frac{1}{\lambda}$$
Variance is:
$$Var(X)=\frac{1}{\lambda^2}$$
Standard deviation is:
$$Stdev(X)=\frac{1}{\lambda}$$

## Normal Distribution
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">normal distribution </mark>is a continuous probability distribution characterized by a bell-shaped distribution function

The formula is:
$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma})^2}$$
Often written in shorthand as:
$$X\thicksim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$$

If you go through the proofs, you find out that the mean, var, and stdev are actually very easy to calculate. The mean, var, and stdev for normal distribution is:
$$E(X)=\mu$$
$$Var(x)=\sigma^2$$
$$Stdev(X)=\sigma$$
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">The normal distribution is symmetric, unimodal (same mean, median, and mode)</mark>

## z-score
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">z-score</mark> is simply the number of standard deviations a single value is from the population mean
	Can only be used when you have the POPULATION
	It converts the raw data into a standard range and enable you to look up the cumulative probability for that value in a table
$$z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$$

The negative table is the total probability up to a given z-score. Basically the left side of the graph if you split it down the middle
The positive table is the total probability up to a given z-score but on the right hand side if you split it down the middle
