## Research Question
What:
* Contribution
* Gives new knowledge
Why?
* Motivation
* Understand its importance
How?
- Comparing with the state of the art / best standards
- Demonstrate your results

Attributes of good research questions:
- Focused on a single problem or issue.
- Researchable using primary and/or secondary sources.
- Feasible to answer within the timeframe and practical constraints.
- Specific enough to answer thoroughly.
- Complex enough to develop the answer over the space of a paper or thesis.
- Relevant to your field of study and/or society more broadly.

## T-Distribution
The <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">t-distribution</mark> is essentially a normal distribution with slightly wider spread (higher standard deviation)
![[Pasted image 20241107115034.png]]
It has its own table of probabilities based on the number of degrees of freedom you have
	Degrees of freedom is n - 1
<mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Probabilities associated with this distribution will be used to reject or accept hypotheses</mark>
This is essentially an adapted z-score
$$t=\frac{\overline{x}-\mu}{\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}}$$
Where overline x is the actual mean, mu is the expected value/mean, s is standard deviation, and n is sample size

t-statistic table: https://www.ttable.org/
![[Pasted image 20241107115523.png]]

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">One-tailed</mark> tests are used if you want to know if one group is BETTER THAN the other
	Typical alpha of 0.025
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Two-tailed</mark> tests are used if you want to know HOW DIFFERENT the two groups are
	Typical alpha threshold of significance of 0.05
	Use two-tailed unless otherwise specified/specific reason
