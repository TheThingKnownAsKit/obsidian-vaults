- **Behaviorism: say what I say**
	- Children learn a language through imitation, reinforcement, and analogy.
	- Stimulus -> response -> reinforcement (Skinner, 1957)
		- Something makes child want to speak -> child speaks -> child is praised/corrected
- **Innate hypothesis: it's all in your mind**
	- Children are equipped with an innate faculty for language
	- They have a language acquisition device (LAD) & universal grammar (Chomsky)
- **Cognitive/Developmental: learning from inside out**
	- Whole development
		- The result of children's interaction with the environment
		- Complementary interaction between developing perceptual cognitive capacities and linguistic experiences (Piaget)
- **Sociocultural: environment facilitates learning**
	- Environment and context is which the language is being acquired matter
	- Vocabulary is bounded by the culture

### 1st language acquisition
|   |   |   |   |
|---|---|---|---|
|Stage|Typical Age|Description/Feature|Example|
|Cooing|3-5 months|Vowel-like sounds|"oh", "uh", "ah"|
|Babbling|6-10 months|Repetitive consonant-like patterns|"ma-ma-ma", "da-da-da", "ba-ba-ba", "na-na-na"|
|Holophrastic/One-word Stage|12-18 months|Single word representing a sentence|"adi" (daddy), "da" (dog), "daw" (down)|
|Two-word Stage|18-20 months|Mini sentences with simple semantic/syntactic relation|"My cookie", "big doggie"|
|Telegraphic Stage|24-30 months|Sentence structures of lexical words, no functional or grammatical morphemes|"Mami shower", "daddy sleep"|
|Later Multiword Stage|30+ months|Grammatical or functional structures emerge|"I want my toy"|
