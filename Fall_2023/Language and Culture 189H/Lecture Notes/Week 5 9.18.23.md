*Content Words*: The words that convey conceptial meaning (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs)
	Open class: new types of content words can be added all the time
*Function Words*: The words that convey grammatical meaning (articles, prepositions, conjunctions, pronouns)
	Closed class: New function words are very rarely added to a language
		e.g. no gender-neutral third person singular pronoun in English, so instead use they instead of choosing between he and she.
The brain treats content and function words differently. Some aphasics are unable to read the function words in and which but can read the content words inn and witch. Children often omit function words from their speech when learning their first language.

*Morphology*: the study of the structure of words and the rules for word formation.
*Morpheme*: the minimal units of meaning
	Can be words on their own, and/or can often be combined with other morphemes to make words.
		e.g. the word book has one morpheme and books has two (book and plural marker)
		https://www.cs.bham.ac.uk/~pxc/nlp/InteractiveNLP/NLP_morph1.html
*Free morphemes* can stand alone. It can be uttered with no other morphemes connected to it.
*Bound morphemes* cannot stand alone, and must be attached to other morphemes.
	*Prefixes*: bound morphemes that attach to the beginning of a root. (un)
	*Suffixes*: bound morphemes that attach to the end of a root (ness)
	*Infixes*: morphemes that are inserted inside a root
	*Circumfixes*: Affixes that attach to both the beginning and the end of a root
		Collectively called affixes.
*Roots*: the morpheme that carries the core meaning and the base upon which other morphemes are attached to create complex word.
	*Bound roots*: Roots that cannot stand alone and can only occur in combination with other morphemes
*Stems*: once an affix has been attached to a root, the result is called a stem to which more morphemes may be attached.
