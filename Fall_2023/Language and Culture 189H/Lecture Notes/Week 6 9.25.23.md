## Evidence for Linguistic Relativity
* Language Structure:
	* Semantic domain (e.g., color, space)
		* How colors are categorized linguistically influence one's actual perception of it. (E.g., Himba tribe -> no identifier for color blue; 19 descriptors for green)
	* Grammatical category (e.g., gender, plurals)
		* Languages vary in terms of obligatory marking of grammatical gender (i.e., whether a noun is 'masculine' or 'feminine')

Sapir argues that:
* Objects or forces in the physical environment become labeled in language only if they have cultural significance--that is, if they "take up the attention of the community"
Whorf investigated that:
* Investigated whether grammatical structures provide frameworks for orienting speaker's thoughts and behaviors
  E.g., Standard Average European (SAE) -> hypothetical grouping of European language that appears to share common modes of thought (because of similar grammatical structures)
  * Influence of language can eb seen both through vocabulary and through more complex grammatical relations
  * Grammatical categories are to some extent obligatory and habitual.
