*Sociolinguistics* is concerned with the relationship between language and the context in which it is used. It studies why we speak differently in different social contexts and functions to convey social meaning.

There are FIVE major fields of sociolinguistics:
* *Varieties of languages*
	* different dialects or variances of a language
* *Language change* (week 11)
* *Language maintenance and shift*
	* non-dominant languages trying to maintain their existence.
	* language being assimilated into the dominant one
* *Language planning*
	* How the government / other institutions try to monitor and control language use
* *Language in contact* (week 13)

**Social factors:**
* *Participants*: WHO are you talking to
* *Social setting*: WHERE are you speaking
* *Topic*: WHAT are you talking about
* *Purpose*: WHY are you speaking

**Social Dimensions:**
* *A solidarity-social distance scale*
	* Concerned with participant relationships. How well do they know someone? High solidarity is intimidate and low is distant
* *A status scale*
	* Concerned with the participant relationships; what is the role of the relative status in shaping linguistics? High status is superiority and low is subordinate.
* *A formality scale*
	* Relating to the setting or type of interaction; useful in assessing the influence of the social setting or type of interaction on language choice
