## Phonetic Change
*Allophone*: One of two or more variants of the same phoneme (doesn't change meaning)
The phoneme inventory of a language can change in several ways:
* It can just disappear. (like |x| in most dialects of English (used to be something like ktha))
* It can be added (Old English did not have the phoneme |3|)
* An allophone may become a separate phoneme (|v| oin Old English used to be an allophone of |f| when |f| occurred between vowels)

*The Great Vowel Shift* Occurred in English between 1400-1600. Seven long vowels of Middle English shifted to an increase in tongue height and the highest sounds.
	| i:  | and | u:  | became the diphthongs | aI  | and | aU  |
* The Early Middle English Vowel Shortening rule -> vowel in the second word of each pair
* The shift -> only long vowels in the first word of each pair
* The Shift -> inconsistencies between spoken and written language, as the latter changes more slowly
## Syntactic Change
Old English followed a SOV word order. (Subject, object, verb)
Modern English is SVO

In Modern English questions are formed by moving the auxilary to the front of the sentence. Old English moved the first verbal element to the front (Kisses the girl the boy often?)
Old and Middle English allowed split genitives in which the words describe the processor occur on both sides of the head noun
	The Wife's Tale of Bath = The Wife of Bath's Tale
	The girl whose sister I'm dating's roommate
