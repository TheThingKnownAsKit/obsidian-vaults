		Individualistic cultures: higher value on the needs and accomplishments of the individual rather than the group.

1. 1st language acquisition stages; 1st/2nd language acquisition key theories (multiple-choice questions)
	Key theories: 
		Skinner: Behaviorism.
			All behavior is learned and that humans enter the world with no innate abilities.
			Children -> clean slates; Language learning -> habit formation (getting linguistic habits printed on clean slates)
			Language is a **conditioned behavior**. Stimulus -> response -> feedback -> reinforcement (positive/negative)
		Chomsky: Innate Hypothesis
			Children are equipped with an innate template for language learning
			Children are born with a **Language Acquisition Device (LAD)**.
			This gives them a set of innate learning tools to acquire language.
			They have a postulated organ of the brain that is supposed to function as a device for learning.
			**Universal grammar** containing principles (universal basic features of grammar) and parameters (the variation across language that determines one or more aspects of grammar)
		Krashen: Input Hypothesis (5 major hypotheses)
			**Acquisition-learning:** subconsciously "picking up" a language vs conscious focus on knowing and applying rules.
			**Monitor:** learner's learned language system acting like a monitor to what they produced. (self correction)
			**Natural order:** acquire rules of a language in a predictable order (grammar)
			**Comprehensible input: (i + 1)** i represents previously acquired linguistic competence, so we move from i to i + 1 by understanding input that contains i + 1
			**Affective-filters**

1. Phonetics/Morphology: being able to understand basic concepts (multiple-choice questions)
	1. **Phonetics**: is concerned with the ways speech sounds are produced, their nature (the physics of sound waves) and how they are perceived
		1. **Phoneme**: a phonological segment that can be phonetically predicted by a rule (/b/ in bit and /p/ in pit)
		2. **Vowel**: the sounds in the production of which no articulators come very close together and the air-stream passes through the vocal tract without obstruction
		3. **Consonant**: the sounds in the production of which there is an obstruction at some of the vocal tract
			![](https://lh4.googleusercontent.com/95id2kHvXX8MVxRORezmZB8lPmaRQCd82fv7BDZOQ49GA0JFZbcyAOFvW0__5zIvyFf8d1xKtUaxy7BDmyMpq7kvoNK4tBpB8mXtMnm2kSq0_mjSyqOTYuk5Mw3rLHPphEi-TmKTKmmJlTEJoFb4xT99=s2048)
			4. **IPA**: The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA). Breaks phonetics into universal chunks that are very easy to apply to other languages.
	1. **Phonology**: is concerned with the ways sounds are patterned (grammar)
	2. **Morphology**: deals with the way the words of a language are structured, how they are made up of smaller meaningful parts
		1. **Content words** are ones they convey conceptual meaning (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs). Can add new ones. **Function words** are the words that convey grammatical meaning (articles, prepositions, conjunctions, pronouns). Cannot add new ones.
		2. A **morpheme** is the minimal unit of meaning of a word. (book has one, books has two). Building blocks. Remember the morphemes don't have to match the exact spelling of a word.
			![](https://lh6.googleusercontent.com/70bJs1KUvWVVfo5Hkl_OsnMe0_3NMAkqGfbM7Iw2Z6KV4GnriEcX5xvTRvv2ld8ilDs9e9VamJC2wGOL_c7GwVtea8tDyVzlcwixd3bjmOFshqFpkqSErtkue1Wbng91TxB0IhfSK6-qEWDJUPeeasyV=s2048)
		3. **Free morphemes** can stand alone. **Bound morphemes** cannot stand alone and must be attached to other morphemes (un, ish).
		4. **Prefix** is a bound morpheme that attaches to the beginning of a root. **Suffix** is a bound morpheme that attaches to the end root. **Infixes** are inserted inside. **Circumfixes** attach to both the beginning and end of a root.
		5. **Roots** are the morpheme that carries the core meaning and is a base. The **stems** are the result after an affix has been attached. **Bound roots** cannot stand alone and can only occur in combination with other morphemes. (ceive in receive, conceive, perceive, etc)
		6. **Derivational morphemes** change the meaning and/or part of speech of a root. Carry semantic meaning and are like affix version of content words.
		7. **Inflectional morphemes** have only grammatical function and never change the part of speech of the root. Always suffixes. (waited, waits, waiting. same word different tense). Are productive so they apply to almost any appropriate root morpheme.
		8. **Lexical gaps** are words that could be in a language but aren't. (unsystem, needlessity, curiosuer, etc)
	1. **Syntax**: is concerned with the ways words go together to form sentences, and how the words are related to one another
	2. **Semantics**: is concerned with the aspects of meaning that are given by words and grammar
	3. **Pragmatics**: handles the aspects of meaning of an utterance that come from its use in a particular context
2. Linguistic anthropology: being able to apply your knowledge/skills in real-life issues/scenario (open-ended questions)
	1. **Linguistic anthropology** is the study of human language as part of human culture, particularly the ways that human beings use languages to create a cultural model of the world and how that model influences the way they live their lives.
	2. Examples:
		1. Some cultures have 15 words for one color but none for another depending on their environment. 
		2. Grammatical categories such as gendered words and plurals.
		3. Language can be a large contributor to how much power or authority someone has. Both in attitude and education ways.
3. Intercultural communication competence: being able to apply your knowledge/skills in real-life issues/scenario (open-ended questions)
	1. **Ethnocentrism** is "our tendency to view our culture as superior to other cultures." It is an "other focused" orientation.
	2. **Intercultural Communication Competence (ICC)**: the ability to communicate effectively and appropriately in various cultural contexts. Key components/factors are:
		1. Motivation: the root of a person's desire to foster intercultural relationships
			1. Intrinsic: healthy curiosity and willingness to learn
			2. Extrinsic: driven by outside reward like money, power, or recognition
		2. Self- /Other knowledge
			1. Self-knowledge: be a good listener and comfortable with uncomfortable
			2. Other-knowledge: direct and thoughtful encounters with other cultures
			3. Mindfulness: social awareness
			4. Cognitive flexibility: continually supplementing and revising existing knowledge
		3. Tolerance for uncertainty
