## Derivative Rules
**Elementary Derivative Rules:**
- Constant function rule: d/dx c = 0 for any real number c
- Constant multiple rule: (kf(x))' = kf'(x)
- Sum rule: (f(x) + g(x))' = f'(x) + g'(x)
- Power rule: (x^n)' = nx^n-1 for any real number n
- Derivative of exponential (natural base): (e^x)' = e^x
- Derivative of exponential (any base): (a^x)' = ln(a)a^x

**The Product and Quotient Rules**
It is NOT TRUE that (f(x)g(x))' = f'(x)g'(x). The derivative of the product of two functions is not the same as the product of two derivatives. It is not the same you can't do that, you have to use the product rule.
If f and g are differentiable functions, then their product P(x) = f(x) * g(x) is also a differentiable function, and:
$(f(x)g(x)^{'} or P^{'}(x) = f^{'}(x)g(x)+f(x)g^{'}(x)$

If f and g are differentiable functions, then their quotient Q(x) = f(x)/g(x) is also differentiable function for all x where g(x) != 0, and
$Q^{'}(x)=\frac{f^{'}(x)g(x)-f(x)g^{'}(x)}{(g(x))^2}$

NOTE: in the product rule, the order of f(x) and g(x) does not matter as long as it is consistent, in quotient it DOES matter.
