## Hyperbolic Functions
$\cosh(x)=\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}$
$\sinh(x)=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}$
You can also think of them as 1/2e^x, 1/2e^-x

$\tanh(x)=\frac{\sinh(x)}{\cosh(x)}=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{e^x+e^{-x}}$

### Properties of Hyperbolic Functions
$\cosh(0) = 1$
$\sinh(0) = 0$
$\cosh(-x)=\cos(x)$
$\sinh(-x)=-\sinh(x)$

### Derivatives of Hyperbolic Functions
$\frac{d}{dx}\cosh(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}$
$\cosh'(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^x-\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}$
OR, literally just sinh. *Derivative of cosh is sinh*.

$\frac{d}{dx}\sinh(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}$
$\cosh'(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^x+\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}$
Or, just cosh. *The derivative of sinh is cosh*.

$\frac{d}{dx}\tanh(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\frac{\sinh(x)}{\cosh(x)}=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{e^x+e^{-x}}$
$\tanh'(x)=\frac{1}{\cosh^2(x)}$ due to the Hyperbolic Identity (quotient rule)
### Hyperbolic Identity
Remember the Pythagorean Identity that says cos^2(theta) + sin^2(theta) = 1
This means that this equation will fall on the unit circle, whose equation is $x^2+y^2=1$

The same is true for hyperbolic functions, except subtract them this time.
$\cosh^2(\theta)-\sinh^2(\theta)=1$
This identity shows us how the hyperbolic functions got their name: the point $\cosh^2(\theta)-\sinh^2(\theta)=1$ lies on the *hyperbola* $x^2-y^2=1$ for any real number $\theta$


## The Tangent Line Approximation
Definition:
Let f be a differentiable function at a point a. Then, for all values of x near a, the *tangent line approximation* of f(x) is
$L(x)=f'(a)(x-a)+f(a)$
Also known as the *linearization* of f(x) at x = a.
* $f(x)\approx f'(a)(x-a)+f(a)$
* The expression $f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)+f(a)$  is also called the *local linearization of f near* x = 1
* We are thinking of a as fixed, so that f(a) and f'(a) are 

It can be shown that the tangent line approximation is the best linear approximation of f near a.
![[Pasted image 20230926113800.png]]
The distance between the black dot on the line and x is L(x).
	L(x) = f'(a)(x-a)+f(a)
The distance between the same black dot and the true value is the *error*.
	E(x)=f(x)-L(x) *EXACT ERROR FORMULA*
	The error will be 0 in a linear function because the linear approximation will follow the graph exactly.
f(x) is the length of the sum of L(x) and error.

### Theorem (Differentiability and Local Linearity)
Suppose f is differentiable at x = a, and E(x) is the error in the tangent line approximation, that is,
$\lim_{x\to a}=\frac{E(x)}{x-a}=0$ 
Simplified limit of E(x) = f(x)-L(x)
This means that the error gets smaller as x gets closer to a. This also means that error is much much much smaller than a.

#### Taylor Remainder Theorem
Allows for extending our notion of estimating to even more general approximations. It is one of the most important and useful theorems in science, engineering, and math.
It is an estimation of error. Will talk about it more in later classes.
$f(x)-L(x)=E(x)\approx \frac{f''(a)}{2}(x-a)^2$ *APPROXIMATE ERROR FORMULA*
If f''(a) > 0 (concave up) we have an *underestimate*
If f''(a) < 0 (concave down) we have an *overestimate*
