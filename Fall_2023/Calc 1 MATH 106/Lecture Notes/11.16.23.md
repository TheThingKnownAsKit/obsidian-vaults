# Differential Equations
An equation of the form $\frac{dy}{dx}=f(x)$ is called a **differential equation**. Note it has a derivative in it. This means the answer is a **function** (not a value such as x = 5).
Finding the **general solution** to the differential equation means finding the general antiderivative y = F(x) + C with F'(x) = f(x)

The antiderivative of dy/dx is just y. The other side is just whatever the antiderivative of f(x) is. $y=\int f(x)dx$
This has infinitely many solutions. You have to narrow it down (explain later). This is why it's a general solution.

If a differential equation has an initial condition, like a given point or y value when x equals something, you can get a specific solution to that condition. The **initial value problem** is the equation and the given condition is the **initial condition**.

Example:
Find the general solution to the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx}=2x$
The antiderivative of 2x is $x^2+C$. The antiderivative of dy/dx is y. So, the general solution is $y=x^2+C$

Now, with initial condition y(0) = 0 solve for a specific solution
$y=x^2+C$
$0=0^2+C$
$0=C$
The specific solution is $y=x^2$

Example: Word problem
Ice is forming on a pond at a rate given by dy/dx = ksqrtt where y is the thickness of the ice, in cm, at time t, in hours since the ice started forming, and k is a positive constant. Find y as a function of t. You may assume there was no ice when the ice first began forming
$\frac{dy}{dx}=k\sqrt t$
No ice, so y(0) = 0
Find antiderivative
$y=k*\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}}+C$
$y=\frac{2k}{3}t^{\frac{3}{2}}+C$
$0=0+C\to C=0$
The solution to the problem is $y=\frac{2k}{3}t^{\frac{3}{2}}$
