# Integrals
Suppose f is continuous for a <= t <= b. The definite integral of f from a to b, written
$\int_a^bf(t)dt$
is the limit of the left-hand or right-hand sums with n subdisvisions of a <= t <= b as n gets arbitrarily large. In other words,
$\lim_{n\to \infty}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(t_i)\Delta t)$
Also for the other hand just change appropriately.
This gives EXACT area under a curve.

EXAMPLE COMPUTING A DEFINITE INTEGRAL:
Determine the value of the following definite integral. The right might be useful (it's just a linear positive graph)
f(x) = 2x + 3
$\int_1^5(2x+3)dx$

The area under the graph makes a trapezoid. The formula for the area of a trapezoid is $\frac{A+B}{2} * h$, where A and B are the lengths of the NOT EQUAL sides (usually top and bottom) and h is the height from bottom to top. In integrals, A and B can be used as the value of the function at the lower limit + value at upper limit
$\int_1^5f(x)(dx)=Area=\frac{f(1)+f(5)}{2}*h$
